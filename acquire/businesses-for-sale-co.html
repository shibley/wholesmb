Businesses for Sale in Colorado: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Colorado's economy runs on two engines that rarely overlap: a fast-growing Front Range tech and professional-services corridor around Denver and Boulder, and a mountain-resort tourism economy in towns like Vail, Aspen, and Breckenridge that runs on an entirely different seasonal calendar. That split creates two very different buying opportunities depending on whether you want a Denver-metro home-service business or a mountain-town hospitality property. This guide covers where Colorado listings live, which industries sell most often, and how to evaluate a deal before you commit.

Where businesses for sale in Colorado are listed

BizBuySell is the dominant aggregator for Colorado and carries the broadest set of listings, concentrated in the Denver metro (Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins, Colorado Springs) with a distinct secondary cluster in mountain resort towns. Mountain-town listings, especially hospitality and outdoor-recreation businesses, often move through relationships with local brokers who specialize in resort-market deals rather than showing up broadly online — worth cultivating if you're targeting Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, or Steamboat Springs. If you're open to running a business that doesn't require physical presence, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can own from anywhere in Colorado.

Popular industries for sale in Colorado

Colorado's outdoor-recreation culture, legal cannabis market, and craft beverage scene shape a business mix you won't see in most states. The most active categories include:

  • Mountain-town hospitality & tourism — restaurants, lodges, ski-rental shops, and event businesses concentrated in Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, and Steamboat Springs, running on a winter/summer double-peak season.
  • Outdoor recreation & adventure tourism — guiding services, gear rental and retail, and adventure-tour operators supported by Colorado's national-park and ski-resort tourism base.
  • Craft breweries & taprooms — Colorado has one of the highest breweries-per-capita rates in the country, and established taprooms with distribution deals come up for resale regularly.
  • Licensed cannabis retail & cultivation — a mature, state-licensed market with established operators, though transfers require state and local regulatory approval.
  • Home services — landscaping, HVAC, and snow removal, all in high demand along the fast-growing Front Range.
  • Tech-adjacent professional services — marketing agencies, IT services, and consulting firms clustered around Denver and Boulder's expanding tech scene.

Colorado markets to watch by region

Denver and its suburbs (Aurora, Lakewood, Centennial) carry the deepest concentration of listings across nearly every category, with Boulder commanding a premium for tech-adjacent and lifestyle businesses given its smaller, wealthier market. Colorado Springs and Fort Collins offer lower entry prices with steady population growth, appealing to buyers who want less competition than Denver proper. The mountain resort corridor (Vail, Aspen, Breckenridge, Steamboat Springs) is an entirely different market — higher price-per-square-foot, extreme seasonality, and a buyer pool that often includes second-home owners rather than operator-buyers, which changes negotiation dynamics.

How to evaluate a Colorado business listing

The fundamentals of due diligence don't change by state, but Colorado has a few things worth noting. Seasonality is more extreme here than in most markets — mountain-town businesses can see 60%+ of annual revenue concentrated in a winter ski season or summer tourist window, so review at least 24 months of month-by-month financials before assuming annual averages apply. If you're evaluating a cannabis business, confirm the state license and local municipal license both transfer cleanly through the Colorado Marijuana Enforcement Division, since local jurisdictions can impose additional restrictions or caps on license counts. Verify any liquor license transfers through the Colorado Department of Revenue's Liquor Enforcement Division, and check commercial lease terms carefully in resort towns, where retail space is scarce and leases can include unusual revenue-share or seasonal-closure clauses. Financing a Colorado acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the most common financing tool for Colorado small-business acquisitions, typically covering 70–90% of the purchase price for businesses with solid financials. Seller financing is common as well — roughly 60% of small-business sales nationally include some seller note, which reduces the cash you need at closing. Note that cannabis businesses generally cannot use SBA financing due to federal illegality, so cultivation and retail cannabis acquisitions typically rely on private lenders, seller notes, or cash buyers. Colorado has a strong base of SBA-preferred lenders concentrated in the Denver metro, well-equipped to handle acquisition loans across the state's dominant non-cannabis industries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Colorado?

BizBuySell is the largest aggregator, concentrated in the Denver metro with a distinct secondary market in mountain resort towns. Local brokers carry many off-market mountain-town listings directly. For online businesses you can run remotely, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Colorado?

The most active categories include mountain-town hospitality and tourism, outdoor recreation and adventure tourism, craft breweries, licensed cannabis retail and cultivation, home services, and tech-adjacent professional services around Denver and Boulder.

How much do businesses for sale in Colorado typically cost?

Most Main Street businesses list from about $100,000 to $700,000. Mountain-town hospitality businesses with real estate or a strong seasonal book can exceed $1.5 million. Pricing is typically 2x–4x seller's discretionary earnings (SDE).

Do I need a business broker to buy a business in Colorado?

Not required, but especially useful for mountain-town hospitality deals and licensed cannabis businesses where local relationships and regulatory familiarity matter. Brokers are paid by the seller. Always engage your own attorney and CPA before closing — Colorado acquisitions often involve cannabis, liquor, or resort-town lease structures that require local expertise.
