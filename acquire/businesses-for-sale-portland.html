Businesses for Sale in Portland, OR: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Portland is a mid-sized metro with a small-business culture that punches well above its population — independent food and beverage, specialty manufacturing, and owner-operated trades are all unusually well represented here. It is also a market where the honest read and the reputational read have drifted apart, which means a buyer who does the work on the ground can find better arithmetic than the national narrative implies. What you cannot skip is the Oregon layer: no general sales tax but a gross-receipts Corporate Activity Tax, a statewide paid leave program, local business taxes stacked on top of state income tax, and a metro that spans two states with completely different tax regimes. This guide covers where Portland-area listings actually live, which categories trade, and the diligence that decides whether an asking price holds up.

Where businesses for sale in Portland are listed

BizBuySell carries the largest public inventory for the metro, but searching the city of Portland alone will miss most of it. The Westside — Beaverton, Hillsboro, Tigard, Tualatin, Lake Oswego — holds a large share of the professional-service, trades, and light-industrial listings. Gresham, Clackamas, Milwaukie, and Oregon City cover the east and south suburbs at lower price points. And across the Columbia, Vancouver and Camas listings show up in Portland-metro searches while sitting under Washington tax law entirely, which is a distinction worth being deliberate about rather than surprised by.

Oregon also has a long-established broker and intermediary community, and a real share of Main Street deals here move through local brokers, CPAs, and attorneys before they ever hit a public portal. If you have a specific category in mind, telling three or four local accountants what you are looking for is a genuinely productive use of a week. For businesses that do not need a physical footprint, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can operate from anywhere — including from a lower-cost part of the state.

Popular industries for sale in Portland

The listing mix reflects an independent-heavy consumer economy, an aging cohort of owner-operators in the trades, and a manufacturing base that never fully left:

  • Food & beverage — restaurants, bars, coffee shops, bakeries, and food carts and trucks. Portland's cart pods are a genuinely distinct asset class, and the pod lease is usually more important than the cart.
  • Building trades — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and painting, supported by an older housing stock and a long wet season that keeps envelope and drainage work permanently in demand.
  • Recurring-service businesses — commercial cleaning, landscaping, pest control, pressure washing, and property management.
  • Healthcare & personal services — dental and veterinary practices, home care agencies, salons, med spas, and gyms.
  • Light manufacturing & specialty production — food production, apparel and outdoor goods, metal fabrication, and contract machining, concentrated in the inner east industrial districts and out along the Sunset corridor. These are frequently the most interesting listings in the market and the ones most often overlooked by first-time buyers.
  • Auto & logistics — auto repair, towing, and moving companies.

Portland submarkets to watch

Central city and inner east — downtown, the Pearl, and the inner eastside quadrants. Downtown storefront and food businesses are still shaped by post-pandemic office attendance patterns, so a weekday-lunch-dependent operation deserves a much harder look at its last twelve months than at its historical peak. The inner east side, by contrast, runs on residential density and has held up better; it is also where most of the small-scale production and maker businesses sit.

The Westside — Beaverton, Hillsboro, Tigard, Lake Oswego — is anchored by the semiconductor and tech employment corridor and carries higher household incomes. It is where B2B services, trades with commercial client bases, and professional practices concentrate, and where sellers are most likely to own their building, which opens the door to an SBA 504 structure on the real estate.

East and south — Gresham, Clackamas, Milwaukie, Oregon City — is the value end of the same labor market. Asking prices and occupancy costs drop while the customer base stays inside the metro economy, and for a first-time buyer this is frequently where the numbers work best.

Across the river — Vancouver and Camas, Washington — appears in most Portland-metro searches. Treat these as Washington acquisitions: no personal income tax, but a business and occupation tax on gross receipts and a retail sales tax. That is a different model, not a rounding difference, and it changes both the seller's historical statements and your own after-tax result.

How to evaluate a Portland business listing

Start with revenue verification, because Oregon removes the usual shortcut. In most states you cross-check a retail seller's claimed revenue against filed sales tax returns. Oregon has no statewide general sales tax, so that return does not exist. Verify instead through bank deposits, merchant processor statements, and filed federal returns, and insist on all three rather than a broker's summary. This is the single most important adjustment an out-of-state buyer needs to make here.

Then map the tax stack the business actually pays. Oregon levies a Corporate Activity Tax on commercial activity above a threshold, which is assessed on gross receipts rather than profit — meaning a low-margin, high-revenue business can owe it in a year it barely earns anything, and a growing business can cross into it after you buy. On top of that, Portland-area businesses commonly face city and county business taxes, and Oregon has state personal income tax. Have your accountant model the combined burden before you agree to a price; buyers relocating from a no-income-tax state routinely misjudge this.

Work the labor line carefully. Oregon has a tiered minimum wage that differs inside the Portland metro from the rest of the state, a state paid family and medical leave program funded by payroll contributions, mandatory sick time, and predictive-scheduling requirements that apply to larger employers in certain sectors including food service and retail. Verify the seller has been compliant with all of it, and check worker classification on anything staffed heavily with 1099 contractors — cleaning, landscaping, delivery, and home services are the usual suspects, and misclassification liability follows the business rather than the seller.

Confirm licenses and permits genuinely transfer. Oregon construction contractor licensing is administered by the state CCB and attaches to the licensed entity, with bonding and insurance requirements you will need to satisfy in your own name. Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC work carries additional individual trade licensure, so establish early whether you or a retained employee can hold it. Liquor licensing runs through the state and requires its own transfer approval. Food carts, restaurants, childcare, and healthcare all have county or state licensure with their own timelines. Pull the last two years of health and building inspection records on any physical-premises business.

Read the lease before the financials. Remaining term, renewal options and who controls them, escalation schedule, assignment consent, and whether a personal guarantee survives. On a food cart, the pod lease and the pod operator's own tenure are the business; a cart with a short runway at a good pod is an option on a renegotiation you have not had yet. Run the full process against our guide on how to buy a business and the due diligence checklist, and price it with the valuation guide.

Normalize for seasonality honestly. Portland's wet season is long, and it moves numbers. Landscaping, painting, roofing, and pressure washing all compress their revenue into a handful of months. Restaurants with patios have a summer that flatters an annual average. Ask for three years of monthly data and underwrite the trough, not the peak.

Financing a Portland acquisition

SBA 7(a) is the standard route for Main Street deals and typically covers the large majority of the purchase price where the books are clean and cash flow covers debt service with room to spare. Oregon has active SBA-preferred lenders alongside community banks and credit unions that genuinely want acquisition paper. Recurring-revenue businesses underwrite best — commercial cleaning, pest control, property management, home care, and managed IT all have contracted, measurable revenue, and they are also the categories least exposed to the foot-traffic questions that make parts of the consumer market harder to underwrite right now.

Where the seller owns the building — common on the Westside and out east — pairing an SBA 504 on the real estate with a 7(a) on the operating business is usually the better structure, because it converts occupancy cost into equity. Seller financing is worth pushing for on anything with customer concentration, lease risk, or a downtown location; a held note or an earnout gives a confident seller a way to prove the revenue survives the handoff. Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Portland, Oregon?

BizBuySell carries the largest public inventory for the Portland metro, and you should search the metro rather than the city alone — a large share of listings sit in Beaverton, Hillsboro, Gresham, Tigard, Lake Oswego, and across the Columbia in Vancouver, Washington. Oregon also has an active broker community, and a meaningful portion of Main Street deals surface first through local brokers, CPAs, and attorneys rather than on a portal. For businesses that do not need a physical location, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can run from anywhere.

Does Oregon's lack of a sales tax change how I evaluate a Portland business?

Yes, in two directions. Oregon has no statewide general sales tax, which removes one common category of seller tax liability and simplifies retail reconciliation. But it also removes a verification source buyers rely on elsewhere, because there are no state sales tax returns to check reported retail revenue against — you verify through bank deposits, merchant processor statements, and filed federal returns instead. Separately, Oregon imposes a Corporate Activity Tax on commercial activity above a threshold, so a growing business can cross into it, and Portland-area businesses face city and county business taxes that out-of-state buyers routinely overlook.

What kinds of businesses sell most often in Portland?

Food and beverage, including restaurants, bars, coffee shops, bakeries, and food carts; the building trades such as HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, and painting; recurring-service businesses like commercial cleaning, landscaping, and pest control; healthcare and personal services including dental, veterinary, home care, and salons; and light manufacturing and specialty production, which is unusually well represented in this metro relative to its size.

Should I look at Vancouver, Washington listings too?

Often yes, but understand that you are crossing into a different tax and regulatory regime, not just a different suburb. Washington has no personal income tax but does levy a business and occupation tax on gross receipts plus a retail sales tax, while Oregon has income tax and no general sales tax. That affects the seller's historical financials, your own after-tax outcome, and where customers choose to shop. Treat a Vancouver business as a Washington acquisition with a Portland labor market, and model it separately before comparing asking prices across the river.

How do I finance a Portland business acquisition?

SBA 7(a) is the standard path and commonly covers the large majority of the purchase price when the books are clean and cash flow supports the debt service. Oregon has active SBA-preferred lenders along with community banks and credit unions that lend on acquisitions. Where the seller owns the real estate, pairing an SBA 504 on the building with a 7(a) on the operating business is usually the stronger structure. Seller financing is worth pushing for on any business with customer concentration or lease risk, because a held note keeps the seller invested in a clean transition.
