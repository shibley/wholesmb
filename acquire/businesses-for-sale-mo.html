Businesses for Sale in Missouri: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Missouri combines a deep manufacturing legacy centered on St. Louis with Kansas City's logistics and agribusiness economy, plus a lower cost of living than most coastal states. That split creates distinct buying opportunities depending on whether you want a St. Louis-area manufacturing or distribution business, a Kansas City logistics or agribusiness operation, or a Main Street service business in one of the state's smaller metros. This guide covers where Missouri listings live, which industries sell most often, and how to evaluate a deal before you commit.

Where businesses for sale in Missouri are listed

BizBuySell is the dominant aggregator for Missouri and carries the broadest set of listings, concentrated around St. Louis and Kansas City with a smaller but steady base around Springfield and Columbia. St. Louis manufacturing and industrial-distribution listings, in particular, often move through relationships with brokers who specialize in the sector rather than showing up broadly online — worth cultivating if you're targeting a production or logistics acquisition. If you're open to running a business that doesn't require physical presence, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can own from anywhere in Missouri.

Popular industries for sale in Missouri

Missouri's manufacturing legacy, central logistics position, and dual-metro economy shape a business mix that's distinct from most Midwest states. The most active categories include:

  • Manufacturing & industrial distribution — St. Louis has one of the deepest manufacturing bases in the Midwest, supporting a steady flow of small and mid-size industrial businesses coming up for sale as owners retire.
  • Logistics, trucking & agribusiness — Kansas City's position along major interstate corridors and its role as an agricultural trading hub drives demand for trucking companies, freight brokerages, and ag-services businesses.
  • Healthcare & home-health services — both metros support a growing base of home health agencies and healthcare staffing businesses as the population ages.
  • Restaurants & hospitality — a steady base across both metros and college towns like Columbia, with restaurant acquisitions concentrated in dense urban corridors and near university campuses.
  • Home services — landscaping, HVAC, and cleaning businesses, all in steady demand across suburban St. Louis and Kansas City.
  • Franchise resales — Missouri's affordable real estate and central location have attracted a dense concentration of franchise operators, creating a steady flow of resale opportunities across food service, fitness, and home-service brands.

Missouri markets to watch by region

St. Louis and its suburbs carry the deepest concentration of manufacturing, distribution, and healthcare-services listings, with valuations reflecting the metro's larger buyer pool. Kansas City (which straddles the Missouri-Kansas border) offers a strong logistics and agribusiness focus along with a growing tech and startup scene that's expanded the local buyer base in recent years. Springfield and Columbia sit between the two — lower competition and entry prices than the major metros, with Columbia benefiting from a stable university-town economy and Springfield anchored by healthcare and manufacturing employers.

How to evaluate a Missouri business listing

The fundamentals of due diligence don't change by state, but Missouri has a few things worth noting. If you're evaluating a manufacturing or industrial business, get an independent equipment appraisal and confirm environmental compliance history, since older industrial sites (common in the St. Louis area) can carry legacy environmental liabilities worth investigating before close. For trucking and logistics businesses, verify DOT/MC operating authority and safety rating through FMCSA's SAFER system. Verify any liquor license transfers through the Missouri Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Control, and check commercial lease terms carefully, since St. Louis and Kansas City rents vary widely by submarket. Financing a Missouri acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the most common financing tool for Missouri small-business acquisitions, typically covering 70–90% of the purchase price for businesses with solid financials. Seller financing is common as well — roughly 60% of small-business sales nationally include some seller note, which reduces the cash you need at closing. Missouri's lower cost of living and commercial real estate costs relative to coastal metros are a meaningful factor for buyers modeling post-acquisition cash flow, since operating expenses tend to stretch further. Both St. Louis and Kansas City have strong bases of SBA-preferred lenders well-equipped to handle acquisition loans across the state's dominant industries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Missouri?

BizBuySell is the largest aggregator, concentrated around St. Louis and Kansas City with a smaller base around Springfield and Columbia. Manufacturing-focused brokers carry many off-market St. Louis listings directly. For online businesses you can run remotely, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Missouri?

The most active categories include manufacturing and industrial distribution, logistics and trucking tied to Kansas City's agribusiness economy, healthcare and home-health services, restaurants and hospitality, home services, and franchise resales.

How much do businesses for sale in Missouri typically cost?

Most Main Street businesses list from about $100,000 to $500,000. St. Louis and Kansas City manufacturing and healthcare-services businesses with strong cash flow can exceed $1 million. Pricing is typically 2x–4x seller's discretionary earnings (SDE).

Do I need a business broker to buy a business in Missouri?

Not required, but especially useful for manufacturing and distribution deals and licensed businesses where equipment valuation and regulatory familiarity matter. Brokers are paid by the seller. Always engage your own attorney and CPA before closing.
