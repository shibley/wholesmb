Businesses for Sale in Phoenix: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Phoenix is one of the better Main Street acquisition markets in the country for a specific reason: sustained population growth, a huge stock of single-family homes, and a climate that makes several service categories non-optional. That combination produces an unusually deep supply of home-service businesses with real recurring revenue. It also produces a seasonality pattern that runs opposite to most US metros, which is the single thing out-of-state buyers most often misread. This guide covers where Valley listings live, which categories move, and the diligence that matters specifically in Arizona.

Where businesses for sale in Phoenix are listed

BizBuySell is the primary aggregator for the metro, and you should search it at the Valley level rather than the Phoenix city limits — Scottsdale, Mesa, Tempe, Chandler, Gilbert, Peoria, and Glendale share the same customer base, labor pool, and broker network, so restricting to one municipality hides most of the inventory. Arizona business brokers handle a large share of Main Street deals directly and often have listings before they hit the aggregators, which makes it worth contacting two or three in your target category. For businesses that don't depend on a Phoenix location at all, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can run from anywhere.

Popular industries for sale in Phoenix

The Valley's listing mix is weighted toward services that a hot, fast-growing, single-family metro cannot do without:

  • Home services — HVAC above all, plus pool service, landscaping, pest control, and plumbing. Summer heat makes cooling and pool maintenance closer to a utility than a discretionary purchase.
  • Auto services — repair shops, car washes, and detailing, serving a sprawling, car-dependent metro with long average commutes.
  • Senior & home care — home care agencies and related non-medical care, supported by one of the largest retiree populations in the country.
  • Food & beverage — restaurants, coffee shops, and bars, concentrated in Old Town Scottsdale, downtown Phoenix, and Tempe's student corridor.
  • Beauty & wellness — salons, med spas, and gyms, strongest in Scottsdale and the East Valley.
  • Storage & logistics — self-storage and last-mile services benefiting from continuous in-migration and household churn.

Valley submarkets to watch

Scottsdale carries the highest commercial rents and the most affluent customer base, so restaurant, salon, and med spa listings there price at a premium and assume you can sustain a high-end positioning. Downtown Phoenix and Tempe skew toward food, bars, and student- and office-driven traffic, with Tempe's revenue closely tied to the university calendar. Mesa, Chandler, and Gilbert are where most of the family-household home-service businesses operate and generally offer the best price per dollar of earnings. Glendale, Peoria, and Surprise cover the growing West Valley, where newer housing stock means fewer repair-driven jobs but rising route density. If you are buying a route-based business, the specific ZIP codes served matter far more than the mailing address on the listing.

How to evaluate a Phoenix business listing

Start with seasonality, and start with the assumption that it runs backwards from what you expect. Phoenix consumer traffic thins out in July and August while HVAC and pool businesses hit their peak; some categories also lean heavily on winter residents who are simply not in the state half the year. A trailing-twelve-month total tells you very little here. Ask for three years of monthly revenue and look at the shape, not the sum.

Licensing is the second Arizona-specific item, and it kills more home-service deals than price does. Contractor work is regulated by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, and a license is generally tied to a qualifying party rather than transferring automatically with the business. Confirm early whether the seller's qualifying party is staying, whether you can qualify yourself, or whether you need to hire someone who can — before you are deep into diligence.

Beyond that: check how much revenue sits in recurring service agreements versus one-time jobs, because that gap explains most of the multiple difference between two superficially similar HVAC or pool companies. Verify technician wages against a genuinely tight trades labor market rather than the seller's historical payroll, and assess how much of the customer base is personally attached to the owner. Financing a Phoenix acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the standard route for acquisitions in this range and typically cover 70–90% of the purchase price where the books are clean and documented. Phoenix home-service businesses tend to underwrite well because contract-backed revenue is easy for a lender to see, but expect underwriters to test debt-service coverage against the slow months rather than an annual average, especially in seasonal categories. Seller financing is common and worth pushing for: a seller carrying a note through your first full summer is making a real statement about the business surviving the transition. Arizona has an active set of SBA-preferred lenders familiar with trades and home-service acquisitions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Phoenix?

BizBuySell has the deepest Valley coverage — search the whole metro, not just Phoenix city limits, since Scottsdale, Mesa, Tempe, Chandler, and Gilbert share one market. Arizona brokers work Main Street deals directly and often have listings early. For remote-run businesses, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Phoenix?

Home services lead by volume — HVAC, pool service, landscaping, and pest control — followed by auto services and car washes, senior and home care, restaurants and bars, salons and med spas, and self-storage.

How much do businesses for sale in Phoenix typically cost?

Main Street businesses commonly list from roughly $100,000 to $750,000, with established multi-truck HVAC, plumbing, and home-service companies frequently exceeding $1 million on the strength of recurring service contracts. Pricing typically runs 2.5x–4x SDE.

What should I watch out for when buying a business in Phoenix?

Seasonality runs opposite to most metros — review three years of monthly revenue, since consumer businesses slow in summer while HVAC and pool service peak. Then confirm Arizona ROC contractor licensing early, since a license follows a qualifying party rather than transferring automatically, and check how much revenue is recurring versus one-time.
