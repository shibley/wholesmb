Businesses for Sale in Tampa: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Tampa Bay has been one of the fastest-growing metros in the country for most of the last decade, and that growth shows up in the listing market: a deep supply of home-service and trade businesses, an older customer base that keeps senior and health services busy, and a steady stream of retiring owners who moved here decades ago and are now selling. The catch is that Florida's cost structure has moved faster than most sellers' P&Ls reflect. Insurance in a coastal hurricane market is the line item that breaks otherwise clean deals, and storm years distort earnings in both directions. This guide covers where Tampa Bay listings live, which categories actually move, and the Florida-specific diligence that decides whether a listing is worth its asking price.

Where businesses for sale in Tampa are listed

BizBuySell is the primary aggregator for Tampa Bay, and you should search the bay rather than the city — St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Largo, Brandon, Riverview, Wesley Chapel, and Lakeland share one labor market, and plenty of service businesses that list as "Tampa" actually run their crews out of a yard in Pinellas or eastern Hillsborough. Florida supports an unusually large business-broker community for its population, which cuts both ways: inventory is easy to find, but a lot of it is priced optimistically by brokers working on volume. Build relationships with two or three brokers who specialize in your target category, because in the trades the better businesses often trade before they are widely advertised. For businesses that don't need a physical Florida presence, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can run from anywhere.

Popular industries for sale in Tampa

The Tampa Bay listing mix reflects year-round growing season, heat and humidity, an older population, and a lot of water:

  • Outdoor & property maintenance — landscaping and lawn care, pool service, and pressure washing, all genuinely recurring here because grass never stops growing and algae never stops forming.
  • Trades — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and roofing, with HVAC carrying the strongest year-round demand of any trade in this climate.
  • Senior & health services — home care agencies, mobility and medical equipment, and chiropractic and dental practices serving one of the oldest metro populations in the country.
  • Food, beverage & hospitality — restaurants, bars, and coffee shops, concentrated in South Tampa, Ybor, downtown St. Pete, and the beach corridors.
  • Auto, marine & storage — auto repair, car washes, boat and marine service, and self-storage, all supported by high vehicle and boat ownership.
  • B2B & professional services — commercial cleaning, property management, insurance agencies, and accounting firms serving a large small-business and rental base.

Tampa Bay submarkets to watch

Hillsborough County — Tampa, Brandon, Riverview, Plant City — holds the deepest inventory of trade and B2B businesses and the most commercial and industrial customer base. South Tampa prices consumer businesses at a premium and turns over restaurants and boutique retail quickly. Pinellas County, through St. Petersburg, Clearwater, and the beach communities, is denser, older, and more tourism-exposed, with strong pool, marine, cleaning, and hospitality inventory but tighter commercial real estate. Pasco County to the north — Wesley Chapel, Land O' Lakes, New Port Richey — is where much of the recent residential growth landed, so home services there are growing but often under-systematized and owner-dependent. Polk County around Lakeland functions as a partly separate, cheaper market with logistics and light-industrial exposure. As with any sprawling metro, confirm where a service business's customers actually are before you assume you can serve them from your side of the bay: crossing the bay in traffic can quietly destroy a route business's margin.

How to evaluate a Tampa business listing

Start with insurance, not the P&L. Commercial property, general liability, and commercial auto coverage in a coastal hurricane-exposed market have repriced significantly in recent years, and a seller's historical premium tells you very little about what a new owner will be quoted — particularly if the seller has a long claims-free relationship with a carrier or is operating under an older policy. Get your own quotes on the actual assets, vehicles, and location before you agree to a price, not during the financing contingency. On a thin-margin business, an insurance delta can consume the entire earnings improvement you were counting on.

Second, normalize storm-year earnings in both directions. Roofing, restoration, tree service, fencing, and general contracting businesses often show a large revenue spike in the twelve to twenty-four months after a significant hurricane, and paying a multiple on that spike is one of the most common ways buyers overpay in Florida. Ask for five years of revenue rather than three, and look at the pre-storm baseline. The mirror image also applies: a restaurant, retail shop, or tourism-linked business may show a depressed year because it was closed or its corridor was disrupted, which can be a legitimate buying opportunity if you can document the cause.

Third, check licensing and worker classification. Florida licenses contractors at the state level through the DBPR, with additional county-level registration for some trades, and a business whose license sits with a departing qualifier is not something you can simply take over — confirm early whether you or an employee can qualify the license, because this kills more Florida trade deals than any other single issue. Then look hard at how the workforce is classified: lawn, cleaning, and construction businesses in this market frequently pay crews as 1099 contractors, and reclassification exposure transfers with an asset purchase more often than buyers expect. Finally, verify the current Florida treatment of sales tax on commercial rent with a CPA, since that rule has changed in recent years and older lease math may no longer apply. Financing a Tampa acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the standard route and typically cover 70–90% of the purchase price where financials are clean and documented. Florida has a deep bench of SBA-preferred lenders, and recurring-route businesses — pool service, lawn maintenance, pest control, commercial cleaning — underwrite particularly well here because their revenue is contracted and their customer churn is measurable. Expect underwriters to ask specifically about insurance cost and storm exposure in this market, and to size the loan off their own assumptions rather than the seller's historicals. Where the deal includes real estate, pairing an SBA 504 with the 7(a) is usually the better structure, and Tampa's industrial and flex space is still cheap enough relative to comparable Sun Belt metros that owner-occupied purchases remain realistic for Main Street buyers. Seller financing is common in Florida and worth pushing for, especially on businesses with a retiring owner whose relationships carry the customer base — a seller carrying paper has a reason to make the handoff work.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Tampa?

BizBuySell has the deepest Tampa Bay coverage — search the whole bay rather than the city limits, since St. Petersburg, Clearwater, Brandon, Riverview, Wesley Chapel, and Lakeland share one market. Florida has a large broker community, so working two or three category specialists is worthwhile. For remote-run businesses, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Tampa?

Outdoor and property maintenance lead — lawn care, pool service, and pressure washing — followed by HVAC and the other trades, home care and senior services, restaurants and bars, marine and auto service, self-storage, and B2B services like commercial cleaning and property management.

How much do businesses for sale in Tampa typically cost?

Most Main Street listings fall between roughly $100,000 and $750,000, with established multi-crew trade companies running past $1 million. Pricing generally runs 2x–4x SDE, and contracted route businesses like pool and lawn service command the higher end because their revenue is predictable and transferable.

What should I watch out for when buying a business in Tampa?

Get your own insurance quotes before agreeing to a price — coastal commercial coverage has repriced and the seller's premium is not a reliable guide. Normalize storm-year revenue using five years of data rather than three, confirm that any state contractor license can be re-qualified under you, check whether crews are classified as 1099 contractors, and verify current commercial-rent sales tax treatment with a CPA.
