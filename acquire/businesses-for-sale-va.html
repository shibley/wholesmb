Businesses for Sale in Virginia: Buyer's Guide 2026

Virginia pairs the stability of the federal-government economy around Washington, D.C. with a business-friendly, right-to-work legal environment. This guide covers where to search, what industries dominate from Northern Virginia to Hampton Roads, what deals cost, and the state-specific quirks — including government-contract novation and ABC license transfer — that shape a Virginia acquisition.

⚡ The Short Version

Where to find listings

BizBuySell carries the deepest Virginia inventory, concentrated in Northern Virginia (Fairfax, Arlington, Loudoun), Richmond, and Hampton Roads/Norfolk. Flippa and Empire Flippers cover Virginia-based online and government-services-adjacent businesses, reflecting the region's professional-services talent pool.

What to expect on price

Virginia Main Street businesses typically sell at 2.5x–3.5x SDE. Northern Virginia commands the top of that range given proximity to federal spending and a high-income buyer pool; Hampton Roads and Richmond generally price lower with less buyer competition.

Why buyers target Virginia

Virginia's economy is anchored by federal government spending — Northern Virginia hosts one of the densest concentrations of government contractors, defense-adjacent firms, and professional-services agencies in the country, giving buyers a stable, recession-resistant category to acquire into. Hampton Roads, home to the largest naval base in the world, supports a distinct cluster of marine services, logistics, and ship-repair-adjacent businesses that don't exist at the same scale elsewhere. Richmond, the state capital, offers a lower cost of entry with a diversified base of restaurants, trades, and professional-services firms.

Virginia is also a right-to-work state with no local income tax layered on top of the state rate in most jurisdictions, which simplifies payroll modeling compared to states with municipal income taxes. The trade-off is cost: Northern Virginia commercial rents and labor costs run well above the state average, driven by proximity to Washington, D.C., and competition for talent from federal contractors and tech firms alike.

Most common business types for sale in Virginia

Virginia's business-for-sale market splits along its three major regions — a federal-contracting corridor, a naval and logistics hub, and a diversified capital-region economy:

  • Government-contracting & professional services — IT services, defense-adjacent consultancies, and staffing firms cluster around Northern Virginia's federal-spending corridor, often selling to buyers who want an existing contract vehicle rather than a cold start.
  • Logistics & marine services — ship repair, marine supply, and freight/logistics businesses are concentrated around Hampton Roads' naval base and port infrastructure, a category with few equivalents outside coastal states.
  • Restaurants & food service — a large, liquid resale market across Richmond, Virginia Beach, and the Northern Virginia suburbs, from independent restaurants to franchise resales.
  • Trades businesses — HVAC, electrical, and plumbing contractors are in steady demand statewide as an aging owner cohort retires.
  • Self-storage & commercial real estate-tied businesses — population growth in the Northern Virginia and Richmond suburbs supports steady self-storage and light-industrial acquisitions.
  • E-commerce & online businesses — Northern Virginia's tech and professional-services workforce feeds a steady pipeline of ecommerce and SaaS listings on Flippa and Empire Flippers.

Where to search for Virginia businesses for sale

Valuation: what Virginia businesses sell for

Pricing follows the same general framework as the rest of the U.S. — a multiple of seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) — but Northern Virginia's proximity to federal spending pushes multiples toward the top of the national range, particularly for government-contracting firms with recurring contract revenue. Main Street service businesses (restaurants, trades, personal services) commonly sell at 2.5x–3.5x SDE in Fairfax, Loudoun, and Arlington counties; Hampton Roads and Richmond deals often price 0.5x–1x lower for comparable revenue given the smaller buyer pool.

Government-contracting businesses are valued differently from Main Street businesses — buyers and lenders weight contract backlog, contract vehicle type (prime vs. subcontractor), and customer concentration heavily, sometimes more than trailing SDE alone.

Financing a Virginia business purchase

SBA 7(a) loans remain the most common financing path for established, profitable businesses, and the Washington, D.C. metro area (spilling into Northern Virginia) has one of the country's most active SBA lending markets. You'll typically need roughly 10% down, solid personal credit, and at least two years of verifiable earnings. Seller financing is common for Main Street deals in Richmond and Hampton Roads, while government-contracting acquisitions sometimes use specialized lenders familiar with contract-vehicle-backed financing.

Virginia-specific due diligence checklist

Standard due diligence applies everywhere, but Virginia has a few state-specific wrinkles worth flagging early:

  • Government contract novation — if the target holds federal contracts, a change of ownership typically requires the contracting agency to formally “novate” the contract to the new owning entity. This can take months and, in some structures, the government can decline to novate — build this timeline and risk explicitly into your purchase agreement and financing contingencies rather than assuming contracts transfer automatically.
  • Security clearance continuity — for defense-adjacent contractors, facility security clearances are tied to the entity and its key personnel; a change of ownership can trigger a re-review by the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency. Confirm clearance status and any pending reviews before closing.
  • ABC license transfer — for restaurants and bars, Virginia Alcoholic Beverage Control (ABC) licenses require a new application and approval under the buyer's name; this is not automatic and can take weeks to months depending on the license type.
  • BPOL local business license — most Virginia localities levy a Business, Professional, and Occupational License (BPOL) tax based on gross receipts; confirm the target's BPOL account is current, since unpaid BPOL tax can complicate an asset purchase closing.
  • Lease and CAM structure — Northern Virginia commercial leases often carry steep annual escalations; review the full lease term and any renewal options, since a short remaining term can materially affect valuation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many businesses are for sale in Virginia?

BizBuySell typically lists several hundred active Virginia listings at any given time, concentrated in Northern Virginia (Fairfax, Arlington, Loudoun), Richmond, and the Hampton Roads/Norfolk metro, with a secondary cluster around Virginia Beach.

What types of businesses sell most often in Virginia?

Government-contracting and professional services firms, logistics and marine services (Hampton Roads), restaurants, trades businesses, and self-storage are among the most commonly listed.

Is Virginia a good state to buy a small business?

Virginia pairs a large, stable federal-government economic base with a business-friendly right-to-work environment. Trade-offs include steep Northern Virginia commercial rents and, for government contractors, a contract-novation process that must be handled carefully at closing.

Can I use an SBA loan to buy a Virginia business?

Yes. SBA 7(a) loans are available nationwide, including Virginia, and the Washington, D.C. metro area has one of the country's most active SBA lending markets.
