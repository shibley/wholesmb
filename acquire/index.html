Buy a Business: Your Guide to Acquiring a Small Business

Buying an existing business is often faster and less risky than starting from scratch — you inherit revenue, customers, and a track record. Our Buy a Business hub walks you through how acquisitions actually work, how to finance them, and where to find businesses for sale, whether you want a laundromat down the street or an online store you can run from anywhere.

ℹ This page may contain affiliate links. See our disclosure.

Why buy a business instead of starting one?

Most new businesses fail in their early years, largely because they never reach profitability. Buying an established business flips that risk: you acquire something with existing cash flow, suppliers, staff, and a customer base. The trade-off is upfront cost — but financing options like SBA 7(a) loans and seller financing mean you rarely need the full purchase price in cash. Roughly 60% of small-business sales involve some seller financing, which keeps the previous owner invested in your success.
