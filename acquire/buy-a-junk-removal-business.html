How to Buy a Junk Removal Business (2026)

Junk removal is one of the lowest-capital service businesses you can buy — a truck, a dump-fee account, and a marketing engine are the core assets — which makes it an accessible entry point for a first acquisition, but also means competition is fierce in most metros. This guide covers what a junk removal business is worth, franchise vs. independent economics, how to find one for sale, and what to verify before you sign.

⚡ The Short Version

What you're buying

A junk removal business is really a marketing and dispatch operation wrapped around a truck and labor. You're buying the review base, Google/local-SEO ranking, commercial accounts (property managers, real-estate agents, contractors), and dispatch/booking systems that generate leads — the trucks and equipment are a small fraction of total value.

What it's worth

Small one- or two-truck operations typically price at 2x–3x SDE, often $150,000–$500,000. Larger multi-truck businesses with recurring commercial contracts and a non-owner manager can command 2.5x–4x SDE, with franchise resales priced somewhat lower per dollar of SDE to account for ongoing royalty obligations.

Junk removal business economics: residential vs. commercial vs. franchise

Understanding the revenue mix and operating model is the single most important step before evaluating any specific junk removal business. Revenue and structure typically split three ways:

  • Residential on-demand jobs: One-off garage cleanouts, furniture removal, and estate cleanouts booked through Google, Yelp, or a franchise call center. High per-job margin but requires constant lead generation to stay booked.
  • Commercial & recurring contracts: Property managers, real-estate agents (eviction/foreclosure cleanouts), general contractors (construction debris), and retail/office cleanouts. Lower per-job margin but more predictable, contracted volume that reduces dependency on daily lead flow.
  • Franchise vs. independent: Franchise operations (1-800-GOT-JUNK?, Junk King, College Hunks Hauling Junk) get call-center lead flow, brand recognition, and operating playbooks in exchange for a 6%–8% royalty plus marketing fees. Independent operators keep full margin but must build local SEO, review volume, and referral relationships from scratch.

A business heavily dependent on a single lead source (e.g., one franchise call center, or one real-estate referral partner) is riskier than one with diversified residential and commercial revenue. Ask for a lead-source breakdown, not just total revenue, for the trailing 12–24 months.

What a junk removal business sells for

Small, owner-operator junk removal businesses (one or two trucks) typically sell at 2x–3x annual SDE, often in the $150,000–$500,000 range. Larger multi-truck operations ($1M+ revenue, a manager handling dispatch independent of the owner, and diversified commercial contracts) can command 2.5x–4x SDE, sometimes higher for businesses with strong online review volume and consistent Google Maps ranking in a competitive metro. Factors that push valuation higher: a mix of recurring commercial contracts alongside residential jobs, a manager or lead dispatcher who isn't the owner, strong review volume (200+ reviews, 4.7+ rating) supporting organic lead flow, and low dependence on any single referral source.

Factors that push valuation lower: heavy dependency on paid ads or a single franchise call center for lead flow, an aging or poorly maintained truck fleet, owner-dependent sales/quoting, and thin bookkeeping that makes it hard to separate true SDE from reimbursed dump fees and fuel costs.

Due diligence: what to verify

Junk removal businesses have unique risk factors beyond standard financial due diligence. Protect yourself with these verification steps:

  • Franchise agreement terms (if applicable): Review the royalty rate, marketing-fund contribution, territory boundaries, and remaining term/renewal conditions. Confirm whether the franchisor must approve the transfer and what fees apply.
  • Lead-source concentration: Request a breakdown of leads by source (organic search, paid ads, franchise call center, referral partners) for the trailing 12–24 months. A business overly reliant on one channel is more fragile than the revenue number alone suggests.
  • Disposal & dump-fee arrangements: Confirm which landfills, transfer stations, and recycling/donation partners the business uses, current dump-fee rates, and whether any volume-discount agreements transfer to a new owner.
  • Truck fleet condition and titles: Get an independent inspection of trucks and trailers, confirm clean titles, and check DOT compliance status if the fleet crosses weight thresholds requiring commercial driver and vehicle regulations.
  • Review platform ownership: Confirm who controls the Google Business Profile, Yelp listing, and any franchise-branded review accounts, and ensure login credentials transfer cleanly at closing — review history is a major driver of ongoing lead flow.
  • Insurance and licensing: Verify general liability and commercial auto insurance are current, and check for any required local hauling permits or waste-transporter registrations in the operating jurisdiction.
  • Labor structure: Confirm whether crew members are employees or 1099 contractors, and review any worker misclassification risk, which is a common issue in the labor-intensive hauling industry.

Financing a junk removal business purchase

SBA 7(a) loans are available for junk removal acquisitions, though lenders will scrutinize lead-source diversification and owner dependency closely given the low barriers to entry in this industry. Expect to put down roughly 10%–15%. Because trucks and equipment are relatively inexpensive compared to trades businesses like HVAC or electrical, total financing needs are often smaller, which can also make this an accessible deal size for a conventional small-business loan or even a large SBA microloan.

Seller financing is common, particularly for smaller independent operations, where sellers often carry 15%–30% of the price to smooth the transition and demonstrate confidence that the customer relationships and review base will hold up under new ownership.

What makes a good junk removal acquisition target

Not every junk removal business is worth buying at any price. The best acquisition targets have: (1) a diversified lead mix across organic search, referrals, and commercial contracts rather than dependency on paid ads or a single franchise call center; (2) a manager or lead dispatcher who isn't the seller, so operations don't collapse post-close; (3) strong online review volume and rating supporting durable organic lead flow; (4) recurring commercial relationships (property managers, real-estate agents, contractors) that provide steadier volume than pure residential on-demand work; and (5) clean, verifiable financials that separate true SDE from pass-through dump fees and fuel reimbursements.

Red flags: the seller personally handles all quoting and dispatch with no documented handoff plan, revenue heavily concentrated in paid-ad spend that could stop working under new management, an aging or poorly maintained truck fleet, and unclear or informal 1099 labor arrangements that could expose the buyer to misclassification liability.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a junk removal business cost to buy?

Small owner-operator junk removal businesses commonly sell for $150,000–$500,000, priced at 2x–3x SDE. Larger multi-truck operations with $1M+ revenue can sell for $800,000–$3M+, typically at 2.5x–4x SDE.

Is buying a junk removal business a good investment?

It's a cash-flow-friendly, low-capital acquisition, but low barriers to entry mean competition is high. Businesses with existing commercial accounts, strong reviews, and dispatch systems carry real defensibility over a bare truck-and-driver operation.

Franchise or independent junk removal business — which is better to buy?

Franchise resales offer brand recognition and call-center lead flow but carry 6%–8% ongoing royalty fees and territory restrictions. Independent businesses keep full margin but depend entirely on the owner-built local marketing and review base.
