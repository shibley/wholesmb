Businesses for Sale in Mississippi: Buyer's Guide 2026

Mississippi's business landscape splits across three distinct regions. The Gulf Coast — Gulfport, Biloxi, and Pascagoula — runs on Huntington Ingalls Industries' Pascagoula shipyard, one of the largest shipbuilders in the country, plus a dense casino-and-hospitality economy that rivals Tunica for the state's gaming revenue. Jackson, the capital and largest metro, anchors state government, healthcare (the University of Mississippi Medical Center is the state's largest employer), and a growing logistics base. Tupelo and northeast Mississippi carry the state's furniture-manufacturing legacy — the region calls itself the upholstered-furniture capital of the country — alongside automotive supply chains feeding the Toyota plant in Blue Springs and the Nissan plant in Canton. Mississippi consistently offers the lowest cost of living and commercial real estate of any Gulf Coast state. This guide covers where to search, what industries dominate, what deals cost, and what makes buying in Mississippi different from Alabama and Louisiana.

What to expect on price

Mississippi Main Street businesses typically sell at 2x–3x SDE, in line with national norms, with real estate and buildout costs among the lowest of any Gulf Coast state. Gulf Coast businesses carry a real hurricane and flood-insurance premium baked into operating costs, while furniture-manufacturing suppliers and automotive-supply businesses are priced heavily on contract durability with their anchor OEM or plant relationship.

Why buyers target Mississippi

The Gulf Coast's economy centers on Huntington Ingalls Industries' Pascagoula shipyard, which builds Navy destroyers and amphibious ships and supports a wide network of marine-supply, precision-machining, and logistics businesses across Jackson and George counties. Gulfport and Biloxi add a second anchor in casino gaming and hospitality — a dozen-plus casino resorts drive steady demand for food-service, cleaning, transportation, and entertainment-support businesses. Jackson's metro economy runs on state government, the University of Mississippi Medical Center (the state's largest employer and only Level I trauma center), and a growing distribution and logistics sector along I-55 and I-20. Northeast Mississippi's Tupelo corridor built its identity on upholstered-furniture manufacturing, and while the industry has consolidated over two decades, it still supports a dense network of component suppliers, fabric houses, and logistics providers, alongside newer automotive-supply businesses feeding the Toyota Blue Springs and Nissan Canton assembly plants.

Buyers should weigh two real trade-offs: Gulf Coast businesses face genuine hurricane and flood exposure, with insurance costs that have risen sharply in recent years and can materially affect normalized operating margins, and outside the three anchor regions, Mississippi's Delta and rural counties have real agricultural business volume but a much thinner buyer pool and slower time-to-close.

Most common business types for sale in Mississippi

Mississippi's business-for-sale market spans marine supply, gaming-adjacent hospitality, agriculture, and manufacturing, but a few categories consistently generate the most listings:

  • Marine & shipbuilding supply — precision-machining shops, marine-parts distributors, and logistics providers serving Huntington Ingalls' Pascagoula shipyard.
  • Casino- & gaming-adjacent hospitality — food-service, transportation, cleaning, and entertainment-support businesses serving Gulf Coast casino resorts.
  • Agriculture & aquaculture — catfish farms and processors in the Delta (Mississippi is the nation's largest catfish producer), plus row-crop equipment dealers and cotton/soybean-adjacent services.
  • Furniture manufacturing & supply — component suppliers, fabric houses, and logistics providers around Tupelo's upholstered-furniture cluster.
  • Automotive supply — parts suppliers and logistics businesses feeding the Toyota Blue Springs and Nissan Canton assembly plants.
  • Skilled trades — HVAC, plumbing, roofing, and electrical contractors supporting the Jackson metro and Gulf Coast.
  • Restaurants & retail — steady turnover of independent and franchise concepts across Jackson, the Gulf Coast, and Tupelo.

Valuation: what Mississippi businesses sell for

Pricing follows the same general framework as the rest of the U.S. — a multiple of seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) — with Mississippi Main Street service businesses commonly selling at 2x–3x SDE, roughly in line with national norms, and real estate and buildout costs among the lowest of any Gulf Coast state. Marine-supply and automotive-supply businesses are priced on both SDE/EBITDA and the durability of their shipyard or OEM-plant relationship, with heavily concentrated single-contract businesses trading at a discount to reflect that risk. Gulf Coast hospitality and gaming-adjacent businesses should be evaluated with insurance costs normalized separately — a spike in windstorm or flood premiums can materially change post-close margins even when top-line revenue looks stable.

Always normalize the financials yourself. Add back the owner's salary to arrive at SDE, and confirm revenue against bank statements and tax returns rather than relying solely on seller-provided P&L spreadsheets — this matters especially for casino-adjacent businesses, where seasonal tourism swings can distort a trailing-twelve-month snapshot.

Financing a Mississippi business purchase

SBA 7(a) loans remain the most common financing path for established, profitable businesses, and both the Jackson metro and Gulf Coast have active SBA Preferred Lenders experienced with marine-supply, gaming-adjacent, and Main Street underwriting. You'll typically need roughly 10% down, solid personal credit, and at least two years of verifiable earnings. Seller financing is common too, especially in agriculture and Main Street niches, where retiring owners often want a gradual, structured transition.

Buyers targeting Gulf Coast businesses should get a windstorm and flood-insurance quote before signing a letter of intent, not after — coastal premiums have risen sharply in recent years and can turn an otherwise attractive multiple into a much thinner one once true carrying costs are known.

Mississippi-specific due diligence checklist

Standard due diligence applies everywhere, but Mississippi has a few state-specific wrinkles worth flagging early:

  • Windstorm & flood insurance — for any Gulf Coast business, get an updated windstorm and flood-insurance quote before closing; coastal premiums have risen sharply and can change the real economics of the deal.
  • Gaming-adjacent licensing — businesses that contract directly with casino resorts (vendors, concessionaires) may need Mississippi Gaming Commission vendor licensing or registration; confirm status transfers or can be re-established under new ownership.
  • Aquaculture & agricultural permits — for Delta catfish and row-crop businesses, verify water-use permits and any USDA or state agricultural certifications transfer cleanly and are in good standing.
  • OEM contract concentration — for automotive-supply or shipyard-supply businesses, request a customer-concentration breakdown; a single large account walking away post-close is the most common way an acquisition underperforms.
  • Local alcohol licensing — Mississippi allows counties to opt in or out of alcohol sales (wet/dry status varies by county and even municipality); confirm license transferability and local restrictions early for restaurant and bar deals.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many businesses are for sale in Mississippi?

BizBuySell typically lists a couple hundred active Mississippi businesses at any given time, concentrated along the Gulf Coast (Gulfport-Biloxi), the Jackson metro, and the Tupelo furniture-manufacturing corridor.

What types of businesses sell most often in Mississippi?

Marine and shipbuilding-supply businesses serving Huntington Ingalls' Pascagoula shipyard, casino-adjacent hospitality businesses on the Gulf Coast, Delta catfish and row-crop agriculture, furniture-manufacturing suppliers around Tupelo, and Main Street restaurants and retail are among the most commonly listed.

Is Mississippi a good state to buy a small business?

Mississippi offers the lowest cost of living and commercial real estate of any Gulf Coast state, combined with anchor industries in shipbuilding, gaming, automotive, and agriculture. Trade-offs include hurricane/flood-insurance exposure on the coast and a thinner buyer pool outside the three anchor regions.

Can I use an SBA loan to buy a Mississippi business?

Yes. SBA 7(a) loans are available statewide, and the Jackson metro and Gulf Coast both have active SBA Preferred Lenders experienced with acquisition financing. Most profitable established businesses with verifiable cash flow qualify.
