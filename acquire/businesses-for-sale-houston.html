Businesses for Sale in Houston: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Houston is one of the few large US metros where an ordinary buyer can still acquire a substantial industrial or service business without coastal-metro pricing. The region's economy leans heavily on energy, petrochemicals, logistics, and healthcare, which produces a listing mix you won't find in Los Angeles or Miami — alongside the usual restaurants, salons, and auto shops. This guide covers where Houston listings live, which categories move most often, and what to scrutinize before you sign.

Where businesses for sale in Houston are listed

BizBuySell is the primary aggregator for the Houston metro, covering Harris County plus the surrounding growth corridors — The Woodlands, Katy, Sugar Land, Pearland, and Clear Lake. Brokers affiliated with the Texas Association of Business Brokers handle a large share of Houston deals directly, and industrial and energy-services businesses in particular often trade through broker relationships and industry contacts before any public listing appears. For businesses that don't depend on a Houston address, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can operate from anywhere.

Popular industries for sale in Houston

Houston's listing mix skews more industrial than most metros of its size:

  • Industrial & energy services — machine shops, fabrication, valve and pump servicing, inspection, and equipment rental businesses tied to the Gulf Coast petrochemical corridor.
  • Home & commercial services — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and pest control, all in year-round demand given the climate and the metro's continued sprawl.
  • Logistics & trucking — freight brokerage, warehousing, and trucking companies serving the Port of Houston and the I-10/I-45 freight corridors.
  • Food & hospitality — restaurants, food trucks, and catering, with high listing volume and correspondingly high turnover.
  • Auto services — repair shops, car washes, and tire and collision businesses supported by a car-dependent, heavily commuting population.
  • Healthcare-adjacent practices — dental, chiropractic, and home-care agencies benefiting from the Texas Medical Center ecosystem and a growing, aging population.

Houston submarkets to watch

Inside the Loop (Montrose, Heights, River Oaks, Midtown) carries the highest retail and restaurant pricing along with the tightest lease terms. The energy corridor along I-10 west and the Woodlands to the north concentrate professional-services and industrial-support businesses with strong commercial customer bases. The Ship Channel and Pasadena/Baytown area is where the heaviest industrial-services businesses trade, often with real estate attached. Suburban growth markets like Katy, Cypress, Sugar Land, and Pearland offer lower entry prices with population growth that supports home-services and consumer businesses.

How to evaluate a Houston business listing

Two diligence items are close to Houston-specific. First, flood exposure: check FEMA flood-zone designation and the property's actual flood history for any deal involving real estate, a workshop, or expensive fixed equipment, and confirm what insurance actually costs and covers. Second, land use — Houston has no formal zoning code, which gives unusual operating flexibility but shifts the burden onto deed restrictions, plat notes, and municipal utility district obligations that you need to read directly rather than assume.

Beyond that, apply the standard checks. For industrial and services businesses, concentration risk matters more than in consumer categories: if two or three commercial customers drive most of the revenue, understand whether those relationships belong to the business or to the departing owner. Review equipment condition and remaining useful life honestly, since deferred maintenance on a fleet or shop floor is a real deduction from the asking price. Verify that any required licenses transfer cleanly, and confirm employee classification and overtime practices. For the complete process, read our guide on how to buy a business.

Financing a Houston acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the most common acquisition financing tool and typically cover 70–90% of the purchase price for businesses with clean, documented financials. Houston's lower price points relative to coastal metros mean more deals fit comfortably inside standard SBA parameters without an unusually large equity injection. Seller notes remain common and appear in a majority of small-business sales nationally — a seller willing to carry paper is signaling confidence that the business survives their exit, which is exactly the question you're trying to answer during diligence. Texas has a deep bench of SBA-preferred lenders experienced with industrial-services and home-services acquisitions specifically.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Houston?

BizBuySell has the deepest Houston-area coverage, spanning Harris County plus The Woodlands, Katy, Sugar Land, and Clear Lake. Texas Association of Business Brokers members work Houston deals directly, and industrial businesses frequently trade off-market. For remote-run businesses, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Houston?

Industrial and energy-services businesses are unusually well represented, alongside home services such as HVAC and plumbing, logistics and trucking tied to the Port of Houston, restaurants and food service, auto services, and healthcare-adjacent practices.

How much do businesses for sale in Houston typically cost?

Main Street businesses commonly list from roughly $75,000 to $700,000, while established industrial-services and home-services companies with fleets and crews often exceed $1 million. Pricing typically runs 2.5x–4x SDE, with premium multiples for recurring commercial contracts.

Is Texas a good state to buy a business in?

Texas has no personal state income tax and relatively light business regulation. Houston has no formal zoning code, which adds flexibility but makes deed restrictions and MUD obligations something you must verify directly. Flood history is the other Houston-specific item to check on any deal with real estate or fixed equipment.
