Businesses for Sale in Miami: How to Find and Evaluate Them

Miami is a high-competition market for business buyers. No state income tax, sustained in-migration, and a steady flow of international capital mean good listings attract multiple offers quickly — and that consumer businesses often carry a tourism-driven seasonality that trailing annual figures can hide. This guide covers where South Florida listings live, which categories move most often, and the diligence items that matter specifically in Miami.

Where businesses for sale in Miami are listed

BizBuySell is the primary aggregator for the Miami metro and covers Miami-Dade plus the Broward and Fort Lauderdale corridor most buyers also search. Brokers affiliated with the Business Brokers of Florida association handle a large share of South Florida transactions directly, and Miami sees an unusual volume of out-of-state and international buyers, which shortens the time good listings stay available. For businesses that don't depend on a Miami storefront, Flippa and Empire Flippers list online businesses you can run from anywhere.

Popular industries for sale in Miami

Miami's listing mix reflects a tourism, trade, and services economy:

  • Food, bars & hospitality — restaurants, bars, and cafes, the highest-volume and highest-turnover category in the metro.
  • Beauty & wellness — salons, med spas, and gyms, supported by high discretionary spending and a year-round appearance-driven consumer market.
  • Import/export & logistics — freight forwarding, customs brokerage, and warehousing tied to PortMiami and Miami International Airport, which anchor US trade with Latin America.
  • Property & rental services — property management, cleaning, and short-term-rental support businesses serving a very large stock of rental and second-home property.
  • Home services — pool service, landscaping, pest control, and HVAC, all with year-round demand in a subtropical climate.
  • Auto services — repair shops, detailing, and car washes serving a dense, car-dependent population.

Miami submarkets to watch

Brickell and downtown carry the highest commercial rents and the most competition for restaurant and service listings, with pricing that assumes a professional daytime population. South Beach and the barrier-island corridor are the most tourism-exposed and therefore the most seasonal. Coral Gables and Coconut Grove pair affluent customer bases with tighter, more expensive retail space. Doral and Hialeah concentrate the logistics, warehousing, and light-industrial businesses tied to trade flows, typically at lower price points per dollar of earnings. Broward and Fort Lauderdale to the north offer somewhat more moderate pricing and are worth searching alongside Miami-Dade.

How to evaluate a Miami business listing

Start with seasonality. A Miami consumer business can earn a disproportionate share of its annual revenue between roughly December and April, so a trailing-twelve-month total tells you far less than three years of monthly revenue side by side. Ask for monthly figures and check whether the slow season is merely thin or actually cash-flow negative.

Insurance is the second Miami-specific item. Hurricane, windstorm, and flood coverage costs have risen sharply across Florida, and a seller's historical premium may not reflect what you will be quoted at renewal. Get your own quote before you finalize a valuation. Beyond that, scrutinize the lease term and escalation clauses in high-rent corridors, verify that liquor and professional licenses transfer cleanly, and assess how much of the business depends on the current owner's personal relationships. Financing a Miami acquisition

SBA 7(a) loans are the standard acquisition financing route and typically cover 70–90% of the purchase price where financials are clean and documented. Miami's higher price points and seasonal cash flow make lenders more attentive to debt-service coverage across the slow months, so expect underwriting to look at monthly seasonality rather than annual averages. Seller financing is common and useful here for exactly that reason — a seller carrying a note through the first off-season is signaling real confidence in the business surviving the transition. Florida has a broad set of SBA-preferred lenders experienced with hospitality and services acquisitions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find businesses for sale in Miami?

BizBuySell has the deepest South Florida coverage, spanning Miami-Dade plus Broward and Fort Lauderdale. Business Brokers of Florida members work Miami deals directly. For remote-run businesses, Flippa and Empire Flippers are the leading marketplaces.

What types of businesses are most commonly for sale in Miami?

Restaurants, bars, and hospitality lead listing volume, alongside beauty and wellness businesses, import/export and logistics companies tied to PortMiami, property and rental-support services, home services, and auto services.

How much do businesses for sale in Miami typically cost?

Main Street businesses commonly list from roughly $100,000 to $850,000, with prime-location restaurants, bars, and med spas in Brickell, South Beach, or Coral Gables often exceeding $1 million. Pricing typically runs 2.5x–4x SDE.

What should I watch out for when buying a business in Miami?

Seasonality first — review three years of monthly revenue, not annual totals, since winter high season can mask thin summers. Then get your own hurricane and flood insurance quote rather than relying on the seller's historical premium, and confirm liquor and professional licenses transfer.
