Shopify and Squarespace are two of the most popular platforms for building an online store — but they take fundamentally different approaches to ecommerce. We've tested both extensively to help you choose the one that actually matches how you sell.

Overview: Two Platforms, Two Different Starting Points

The Shopify vs Squarespace comparison is one of the most searched ecommerce matchups — and for good reason. Both platforms can power an online store, but they were built with fundamentally different priorities, and understanding that difference is the key to choosing correctly.

Shopify is an ecommerce platform that also builds websites. Founded in 2006 by Tobias Lütke, Daniel Weinand, and Scott Lake, Shopify started because Lütke wanted to sell snowboards online and couldn't find a good platform. Its DNA is selling products online. Everything — the admin dashboard, the app ecosystem, the checkout flow, the analytics — is designed around helping merchants sell more. Today, Shopify powers over 4.4 million stores worldwide and processed over $235 billion in gross merchandise volume (GMV) in 2024. It's the backbone of brands like Allbirds, Gymshark, Kylie Cosmetics, and millions of smaller shops.

Squarespace is a website builder that also does ecommerce. Founded in 2003 by Anthony Casalena in his dorm room at the University of Maryland, Squarespace started as a tool for building beautiful websites — blogs, portfolios, and business sites. Its DNA is design and content. Ecommerce capabilities were added over time and have grown substantially, but the platform's heart is still about creating visually stunning websites. Squarespace went public in 2021 and powers millions of websites, with particularly strong adoption among creatives, photographers, restaurants, and small businesses that prioritize aesthetics.

Here's the uncomfortable truth that most comparison articles won't tell you: if you already know which type of business you're building, the choice is usually obvious. A dedicated online store selling 50+ products with plans to scale → Shopify. A photographer's portfolio with a small print shop → Squarespace. A restaurant needing a website with online ordering → Squarespace. A dropshipping business → Shopify. The grey area only exists for businesses that genuinely sit between "website that sells" and "store with content."

Both platforms are fully hosted SaaS solutions — meaning you don't need to worry about web hosting, security patches, or server maintenance. You pay a monthly fee, and the platform handles everything technical. Both offer SSL certificates, 24/7 support, and mobile-responsive designs. The differences lie in their ecommerce depth, design flexibility, app ecosystems, and pricing structures.

What Is Shopify?

Shopify is the world's leading ecommerce platform, powering online stores in over 175 countries. At its core, Shopify provides everything you need to start, run, and grow an online business — from building your storefront to processing payments, shipping orders, and marketing to customers.

The Shopify Product Suite

  • Online Store — Build a fully customizable storefront using Shopify themes (free and premium). Drag-and-drop editor, custom code access via Liquid (Shopify's templating language), and support for custom domains.
  • Shopify Payments — Built-in payment processing powered by Stripe. Accept credit/debit cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Shop Pay (Shopify's accelerated checkout). No additional transaction fees when using Shopify Payments.
  • Shopify POS — Point-of-sale system for in-person selling. POS Lite is included on all plans; POS Pro ($89/month per location) adds advanced inventory, staff management, and omnichannel features. Hardware ranges from $49 card readers to full countertop setups.
  • Shopify Markets — Sell internationally with localized pricing, multi-currency, multi-language storefronts, automatic duty and tax calculation, and country-specific domains. Supports selling in 150+ countries.
  • Sales Channels — Sell on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, Amazon, eBay, Google Shopping, and Walmart Marketplace — all managed from your Shopify admin.
  • Shopify Fulfillment Network — Warehousing and fulfillment service for eligible merchants, with 2-day delivery across the US.
  • Shopify Email — Built-in email marketing with templates, automation, segmentation, and analytics. First 10,000 emails per month are free.
  • Shopify Flow — Workflow automation tool (available on Grow and Advanced plans). Create automated rules for inventory, fraud, customer tagging, and order management.
  • Shopify Analytics — Sales dashboards, product reports, customer analytics, marketing attribution, and financial reports. Advanced plans get custom report builders.
  • Sidekick (AI) — Shopify's AI assistant that helps with store setup, product descriptions, marketing copy, and business insights.
  • Shopify App Store — Over 8,000 apps for extending functionality — dropshipping, reviews, loyalty programs, subscriptions, upselling, SEO tools, accounting integrations, and much more.

Who Uses Shopify?

  • Direct-to-consumer brands — From startup brands to Gymshark, Allbirds, and Kylie Cosmetics.
  • Dropshippers — Shopify is the default platform for dropshipping businesses, thanks to its app ecosystem.
  • Multi-channel sellers — Merchants selling on their website, social media, and marketplaces simultaneously.
  • Brick-and-mortar + online — Retailers using Shopify POS to unify in-person and online sales.
  • Enterprise brands — Shopify Plus (starting at $2,300/month) powers enterprise commerce for brands like Heinz, Staples, Red Bull, and Fashion Nova.

What Is Squarespace?

Squarespace is an all-in-one website building platform known for its award-winning templates and design-forward approach. It serves everyone from bloggers and artists to restaurants and online retailers, with ecommerce capabilities that have grown significantly since the platform's early days as a content-focused site builder.

The Squarespace Ecosystem

  • Website Builder — Drag-and-drop visual editor with 150+ professionally designed templates. Sections-based editor lets you build pages from pre-designed content blocks. Full CSS/JavaScript customization on Core plan and above.
  • Ecommerce — Sell physical and digital products, subscriptions, and services. Product pages with variants, inventory tracking, and built-in shipping calculators. Transaction fees vary by plan (2% on Basic, 0% on Plus and Advanced).
  • Squarespace Payments — Built-in payment processing (powered by Stripe). Available in the US, UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand. Also supports PayPal, Afterpay/Clearpay, and manual payment options.
  • Scheduling (Acuity) — Online appointment scheduling with automated reminders, intake forms, payment collection, and calendar integrations. Separate subscription from the website plan.
  • Email Marketing — Built-in email campaigns with templates matching your website's design. Automation, segmentation, and analytics included.
  • Member Areas — Create gated content for subscribers or paying members. Sell digital content, courses, or exclusive access.
  • Video Studio (Unfold) — Create social media content and stories directly within Squarespace.
  • Domains — Register and manage domains directly. Free custom domain for one year with annual plans.
  • Blogging — Rich blog editor with multiple layout options, contributor management, podcast hosting support, categories, tags, scheduling, and RSS.
  • SEO Tools — Built-in SEO management with clean URLs, automatic sitemaps, 301 redirects, meta tag editing, and social sharing options.
  • Extensions — A small but growing marketplace of ~40-50 third-party integrations for shipping, accounting, inventory, and marketing.

Who Uses Squarespace?

  • Creatives and freelancers — Photographers, designers, artists, and writers who need stunning portfolio sites.
  • Restaurants and hospitality — Restaurants, cafes, and hotels using Squarespace for menus, reservations, and online ordering.
  • Small retail shops — Boutiques and artisans selling a curated selection of products.
  • Service businesses — Consultants, coaches, and therapists using scheduling and member areas.
  • Content creators — Bloggers, podcasters, and musicians building their online presence with content at the center.

Pricing Verdict: Head-to-Head Value

Let's compare equivalent tiers side by side:

  • Entry-level website: Squarespace Basic ($16/mo annual) is cheaper than Shopify Basic ($29/mo annual) and better for non-ecommerce websites. But the 2% commerce transaction fee on Squarespace Basic makes it poor for stores.
  • Entry-level ecommerce: Squarespace Core ($23/mo annual) vs Shopify Basic ($29/mo annual). Squarespace is $72/yr cheaper, but Shopify includes unlimited staff, POS, 8,000+ apps, and significantly better ecommerce tools. Shopify's $6/mo premium pays for itself in functionality.
  • Mid-tier ecommerce: Squarespace Plus ($39/mo annual) vs Shopify Grow ($79/mo annual). Shopify is $480/yr more expensive but adds automation (Shopify Flow), 5 staff accounts, lower credit card rates (2.7% vs Squarespace Payments rates), and vastly more integrations.
  • Advanced ecommerce: Squarespace Advanced ($99/mo annual) vs Shopify Advanced ($299/mo annual). Shopify is $2,400/yr more, but the feature gap is massive — custom reports, 15 staff accounts, lowest credit card rates, third-party calculated shipping, and tools designed for high-volume merchants.

Bottom line: Squarespace is cheaper at every tier, but Shopify offers dramatically more ecommerce functionality per dollar spent. For stores selling more than $5,000/month, Shopify's lower transaction fees and superior selling tools typically make up the price difference — and then some.

Design & Templates

This is Squarespace's crown jewel. If design quality is your top priority, Squarespace has a genuine advantage.

Squarespace Templates & Design

Squarespace offers 150+ professionally designed templates, and they are, frankly, gorgeous. Every template is created by Squarespace's in-house design team, ensuring consistent quality. Key design strengths:

  • Visual editor — True drag-and-drop editing with real-time preview. Move sections, resize images, adjust spacing, and see exactly how your site will look.
  • Section-based design — Build pages from pre-designed, customizable sections (hero banners, galleries, product grids, testimonials, CTAs). Mix and match across any template.
  • Consistent quality — Because all templates are first-party, there's no quality lottery. Every template is responsive, well-coded, and visually polished.
  • Typography and color — Advanced typography controls with access to Google Fonts and Adobe Fonts. Global color palettes that update across your entire site.
  • Gallery and portfolio layouts — Squarespace's image gallery and portfolio layouts are the best in the industry. Lightbox, grid, slideshow, masonry — all beautifully implemented.
  • Custom CSS/JS — Available on Core plan and above for deeper customization.

Shopify Themes & Design

Shopify's Theme Store offers 12 free themes and 100+ premium themes ($150-$380 one-time purchase). Shopify themes are functional and modern, but design takes a backseat to commerce:

  • Theme editor — Sections-based editor that's capable but not as fluid as Squarespace's. You customize within the theme's structure rather than freely placing elements.
  • Online Store 2.0 — Shopify's updated architecture allows sections on every page (not just the homepage), app blocks within sections, and more flexibility than earlier versions.
  • Quality range — Free themes (like Dawn) are good but basic. Premium themes offer more design options and features. Third-party themes vary in quality.
  • Liquid templating — Developers can customize any aspect of a Shopify theme using Liquid, HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. More powerful than Squarespace's custom code options but requires development skills.
  • Third-party themes — The Shopify theme ecosystem extends beyond the official store, with marketplaces like ThemeForest offering hundreds of additional options (varying quality).

Design Verdict

Squarespace wins for design quality and ease of visual editing. If your brand is built on aesthetics — if the visual experience of your website matters as much as what you're selling — Squarespace gives you better tools to achieve that vision without hiring a designer. Shopify's themes are perfectly adequate and improving, but "adequate" isn't the same as "stunning."

That said, Shopify's design capabilities are better than they get credit for. Themes like Dawn, Refresh, and Crave are modern and attractive. For most ecommerce stores, the product photography and brand assets matter more than the template's baseline design.

Payment Processing

Both platforms have built-in payment processing powered by Stripe, but the implementations differ significantly.

Shopify Payments

  • Processing rates: 2.9% + 30¢ (Basic), 2.7% + 30¢ (Grow), 2.5% + 30¢ (Advanced)
  • Payment methods: Credit/debit cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, PayPal, Amazon Pay, Meta Pay, plus local payment methods via Shopify Markets
  • Shop Pay: Shopify's accelerated checkout — one-click buying for returning customers across any Shopify store. Shop Pay boasts a 91% checkout-to-order rate, significantly higher than guest checkout
  • Third-party gateways: 100+ supported payment providers (Authorize.net, 2Checkout, WorldPay, etc.) with an additional 0.6%-2% transaction fee
  • Shopify Payments availability: Available in 23 countries including US, Canada, UK, EU, Australia, Japan, Singapore, and more

Squarespace Payments

  • Processing rates: Standard Stripe-based rates, decreasing with higher-tier plans
  • Payment methods: Credit/debit cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Afterpay/Clearpay, PayPal
  • Squarespace Payments availability: US, UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada, and New Zealand
  • Third-party gateways: Stripe and PayPal are the primary options. Far fewer gateway choices than Shopify
  • Transaction fees: Additional 2% commerce fee on the Basic plan (on top of payment processing fees). No additional fees on Core and above

Payment Verdict

Shopify wins for payment flexibility. More gateway options, more payment methods, Shop Pay's accelerated checkout, and availability in more countries. Shopify's decreasing credit card rates on higher plans also reward growing businesses. Squarespace's payment options are adequate but more limited, and the additional 2% commerce fee on the Basic plan is a hidden cost that makes that tier poor for serious selling.

Apps, Extensions & Integrations

This is one of the starkest differences between the two platforms — and a potential dealbreaker depending on your needs.

Shopify App Store: 8,000+ Apps

Shopify's App Store is the largest ecommerce app ecosystem in the world. With over 8,000 apps, there's a solution for virtually any business need:

  • Marketing: Klaviyo, Mailchimp, Omnisend, Privy, Smile.io (loyalty), ReferralCandy
  • Reviews: Judge.me, Loox, Stamped, Yotpo, Trustpilot
  • Dropshipping: DSers, Spocket, Zendrop, Printful, Printify
  • Subscriptions: Recharge, Bold Subscriptions, Seal Subscriptions
  • Upselling: ReConvert, Bold Upsell, Frequently Bought Together
  • SEO: Yoast SEO, SEO Manager, Plug in SEO, JSON-LD for SEO
  • Accounting: QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks direct integrations
  • Customer service: Gorgias, Zendesk, Tidio, Re:amaze
  • Social proof: Fomo, ProveSource, TrustBadge
  • Page building: PageFly, Shogun, GemPages (if you want Squarespace-level design flexibility)

The tradeoff: some merchants end up paying $50-200+/month in app subscriptions, which adds significantly to the total cost of running a Shopify store.

Squarespace Extensions: ~40-50

Squarespace's Extensions marketplace is small but growing. Available integrations include:

  • Shipping: ShipStation, Shippo, EasyShip
  • Accounting: QuickBooks, FreshBooks, TaxJar
  • Inventory: Trunk (multi-channel inventory sync)
  • Marketing: Mailchimp (Core+ plan), Zapier (Core+ plan)
  • Print-on-demand: Printful, Printify (limited integration)
  • Scheduling: Acuity Scheduling (Squarespace-owned, separate subscription)
  • Restaurants: OpenTable, Tock

The limited extension ecosystem means two things: lower add-on costs (you won't spend $200/month on apps) but also fewer options when you need specialized functionality. If your business outgrows what Squarespace does natively, you may find yourself stuck.

Integrations Verdict

Shopify wins overwhelmingly. 8,000+ apps vs ~50 extensions isn't a competition. If you think you might need specialized functionality in the future — dropshipping, advanced loyalty programs, custom subscription billing, specific marketing automations — Shopify's ecosystem gives you options that Squarespace simply can't match. The tradeoff is that Shopify's app model can get expensive, and too many apps can slow down your store.

Point of Sale (POS) & In-Person Selling

Shopify wins by default — Squarespace doesn't have a POS system.

Shopify POS is a full-featured point-of-sale system that lets you sell in person at retail stores, pop-up shops, markets, and events. It syncs with your online store in real time, so inventory, orders, and customers are always unified:

  • POS Lite (included with all Shopify plans) — Mobile POS with card reader. Accept tap, chip, swipe, and mobile wallets. Basic sales tracking and receipt management.
  • POS Pro ($89/month per location) — Full retail POS with staff permissions, inventory management, customer profiles, exchanges/returns, custom receipts, and advanced reporting.
  • Hardware — Tap & Chip Reader ($49), POS Terminal (countertop, $349), and various accessories like barcode scanners and receipt printers.
  • Omnichannel features — Buy online, pick up in store (BOPIS). Browse in store, ship to customer. Local delivery. Gift cards work across all channels.

If you sell both online and in person, this is a significant Shopify advantage. Squarespace users who need POS functionality would need to use a separate system (like Square) and manage two separate platforms for inventory and orders.

Dropshipping & Print-on-Demand

If dropshipping is your business model, this is a one-platform conversation.

Shopify for Dropshipping

Shopify is the undisputed king of dropshipping platforms. The ecosystem includes:

  • DSers — The #1 AliExpress dropshipping app. Bulk order processing, supplier management, automatic price/stock syncing.
  • Spocket — US/EU-based suppliers for faster shipping times. Branded invoicing and real-time inventory sync.
  • Zendrop — Automated fulfillment with US warehousing, custom branding, and subscription box support.
  • Printful / Printify — Print-on-demand for custom t-shirts, mugs, phone cases, and more. Auto-fulfillment with no inventory needed.
  • CJ Dropshipping — Comprehensive supplier network with warehousing, custom packaging, and product sourcing.

All of these apps integrate directly with Shopify — products sync to your store, orders auto-forward to suppliers, and tracking numbers update automatically.

Squarespace for Dropshipping

Squarespace has limited dropshipping support. Printful and Printify offer basic Squarespace integrations, but options for AliExpress-style dropshipping are essentially nonexistent. There's no equivalent to DSers, Spocket, or CJ Dropshipping for Squarespace.

Dropshipping Verdict

Shopify wins decisively. If dropshipping or print-on-demand is your business model, Shopify is the only viable choice between these two platforms. Squarespace was not designed for dropshipping and lacks the integrations to support it at scale.

Ease of Use

Both platforms target non-technical users, but they're easy in different ways.

Squarespace: Beautiful Simplicity

Squarespace is designed for people who want to build a beautiful website without any technical knowledge. The experience:

  • Template selection → Browse 150+ templates organized by industry. Pick one that matches your vision.
  • Visual editor → Drag, drop, click to edit. See changes in real-time. No preview needed.
  • Content first → Adding pages, blog posts, galleries, and text feels natural. The editor resembles modern document editors.
  • Guided setup → Step-by-step prompts walk you through connecting a domain, adding content, and configuring your site.

Squarespace's limitation: when you want to do something the platform doesn't natively support, you hit a wall. Custom functionality requires CSS/JS knowledge or a workaround, and options are limited compared to Shopify's app ecosystem.

Shopify: Commerce-Focused Workflow

Shopify is designed for merchants. The admin dashboard centers around orders, products, customers, and analytics:

  • Product setup → Adding products, variants, pricing, and inventory is straightforward and optimized for efficiency.
  • Order management → Dashboard shows orders, fulfillment status, and customer info at a glance.
  • Theme customizer → Sections-based editor for visual changes. Less fluid than Squarespace but functional.
  • Guided onboarding → Shopify's setup wizard walks new merchants through adding products, customizing themes, setting up payments, and configuring shipping.
  • Sidekick AI → Shopify's AI assistant helps with store setup, product descriptions, and business questions.

Shopify's learning curve is steeper because there are more features to learn. But the ecommerce-specific workflow means that once you're comfortable, managing your store is faster and more efficient than on a general website builder.

Ease of Use Verdict

For general website building: Squarespace is easier. The visual editor is more intuitive, templates are more polished out of the box, and non-commerce tasks (blogging, portfolios, content pages) feel more natural.

For ecommerce operations: Shopify is easier. Adding products, managing orders, configuring shipping, and handling the day-to-day of running a store is more streamlined in Shopify's commerce-focused admin.

Shopify: Pros & Cons

✅ Shopify Pros

  • Purpose-built for ecommerce — Every feature, every design decision, every tool is optimized for selling. It's not a website builder pretending to do ecommerce.
  • 8,000+ apps — The largest ecommerce app ecosystem. Whatever you need, there's probably an app for it.
  • Multi-channel selling — Sell on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Amazon, eBay, Google, and more from one admin panel.
  • Built-in POS — Unified in-person and online selling. Inventory, orders, and customers sync across all channels.
  • International selling — Shopify Markets makes global commerce accessible with localized storefronts, currencies, languages, and tax calculation.
  • Shop Pay — Accelerated checkout with 91% conversion rate. A significant competitive advantage for conversion optimization.
  • Shipping discounts — Up to 88% off USPS, UPS, and DHL rates. Real savings that add up fast.
  • Scalability — From $5/month Starter to Shopify Plus ($2,300+/month) for enterprise. Grows with your business without re-platforming.
  • Dropshipping ecosystem — The best platform for dropshipping and print-on-demand businesses.

❌ Shopify Cons

  • More expensive at equivalent tiers — Shopify Basic ($29/mo annual) vs Squarespace Core ($23/mo annual). The gap widens at higher tiers.
  • App dependency (and cost) — Many features require paid apps, which can add $50-200+/month to your costs.
  • Design limitations — Themes are functional but not as visually stunning as Squarespace's templates out of the box. The editor is less visually intuitive.
  • URL structure — Mandatory /collections/ and /products/ URL prefixes that can't be removed. Minor SEO annoyance.
  • Blogging is basic — Shopify's blog works but lacks the rich content management features of a platform like Squarespace or WordPress.
  • Transaction fees on third-party gateways — Using any payment provider other than Shopify Payments incurs an additional 0.6-2% fee.
  • Content pages feel secondary — Non-product pages (About, Contact, blog) get less design attention than the store itself.

Squarespace: Pros & Cons

✅ Squarespace Pros

  • Best-in-class design — 150+ professionally designed templates that are consistently beautiful. The visual editor is the industry benchmark.
  • Lower entry price — Starts at $16/month (annual) vs Shopify's $29/month. Better value if you don't need deep ecommerce features.
  • All-in-one simplicity — Website, blog, ecommerce, scheduling, email marketing, and member areas in one platform with no apps needed.
  • Superior blogging — Rich text editor, podcast support, multiple layouts, contributor management, and RSS. The best blog for an ecommerce platform.
  • No app costs — Most functionality is built in, so you don't accumulate $100+/month in app subscriptions.
  • Portfolio and gallery layouts — The absolute best platform for photographers, artists, designers, and anyone showcasing visual work.
  • Scheduling integration — Acuity Scheduling (Squarespace-owned) is best-in-class for appointment-based businesses.
  • Content + commerce — If your business is content-first with some selling on the side, Squarespace balances both better than Shopify.

❌ Squarespace Cons

  • Ecommerce is secondary — The platform was built for websites first. Ecommerce features are good but not as deep or flexible as Shopify's.
  • Tiny extension marketplace — ~50 extensions vs Shopify's 8,000+ apps. When you outgrow built-in features, options are limited.
  • No POS system — Can't sell in person through Squarespace. Need a separate system for brick-and-mortar.
  • No dropshipping ecosystem — Virtually no support for AliExpress-style dropshipping. Only basic Printful/Printify integration.
  • Limited multi-channel selling — No direct Amazon, eBay, Walmart, or TikTok Shop integrations.
  • Weaker international selling — No multi-language support, no automatic duty/tax calculation, no localized checkout.
  • Transaction fees on Basic plan — The 2% commerce fee on the cheapest plan makes it a poor choice for stores selling meaningful volume.
  • Scalability ceiling — As your store grows, you're more likely to outgrow Squarespace's capabilities than Shopify's.

Alternatives Worth Considering

If neither Shopify nor Squarespace feels quite right, here are three alternatives:

WooCommerce (WordPress)

Free, open-source ecommerce plugin for WordPress. Maximum flexibility and customization — you control everything, including hosting, design, and functionality. The tradeoff is complexity: you need WordPress hosting, security management, and technical skills (or a developer). Best for businesses that want total control and are comfortable with WordPress. WooCommerce powers 36% of all online stores, more than any other platform.

Wix

A website builder similar to Squarespace with growing ecommerce capabilities. Wix offers a more flexible editor (true drag-and-drop with pixel-level positioning) and competitive pricing (ecommerce plans from $27/month). Its App Market has ~800 apps — far more than Squarespace but still a fraction of Shopify's. Best for businesses that want Squarespace-level design with more customization flexibility.

BigCommerce

An enterprise-grade ecommerce platform that's a direct Shopify competitor. BigCommerce offers strong built-in features (no transaction fees on any plan, multi-channel selling, B2B functionality) and scales well for high-volume merchants. Less popular than Shopify (smaller app ecosystem and community) but competitive on features and pricing. Best for mid-to-large businesses that want Shopify-level ecommerce without the app dependency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Shopify better than Squarespace for ecommerce?

For serious, growth-oriented ecommerce, yes. Shopify is purpose-built for online selling with 8,000+ apps, multi-channel selling, POS, dropshipping support, and international commerce tools. Squarespace is a website builder with ecommerce capabilities — excellent for small shops but not designed for high-volume or complex selling. If selling products is your primary business, Shopify wins.

Is Squarespace cheaper than Shopify?

At every tier, yes — Squarespace Basic starts at $16/month (annual) vs Shopify Basic at $29/month. However, Squarespace's cheapest plan adds a 2% commerce transaction fee, and its ecommerce features are more limited. For serious stores, Shopify's additional cost often pays for itself in superior selling tools and lower transaction fees when using Shopify Payments.

Can you dropship with Squarespace?

Technically yes, but it's very limited. Squarespace has basic Printful/Printify integrations for print-on-demand, but no support for AliExpress-style dropshipping. Shopify has dozens of dedicated dropshipping apps (DSers, Spocket, Zendrop) that make it the only realistic choice for dropshipping businesses.

Which platform has better SEO?

Both handle SEO fundamentals well. Squarespace has a slight edge in built-in features (cleaner URLs, built-in redirects, better schema markup, superior blogging). Shopify has better extensibility via SEO apps and generally faster page speeds. Neither will hold back your SEO — content quality matters far more than platform differences.

Does Squarespace have an app store?

Squarespace has an Extensions marketplace with roughly 40-50 integrations. Shopify's App Store has 8,000+. If you anticipate needing specialized functionality (dropshipping, reviews, advanced loyalty, custom subscriptions), Shopify's ecosystem is vastly larger.

Can I sell internationally on Squarespace?

Squarespace supports multi-currency selling through Squarespace Payments, but lacks multi-language support, automatic duty/tax calculation, and localized checkout experiences. Shopify Markets provides all of these for 150+ countries. For serious international selling, Shopify is significantly stronger.

Which is easier to use?

For general website building and design, Squarespace is easier — its visual editor is more intuitive. For ecommerce operations (adding products, managing orders, configuring shipping), Shopify is more streamlined. The "easier" platform depends on whether you're primarily building a website or running a store.

Can I switch from Squarespace to Shopify?

Yes. Shopify offers a built-in import tool for Squarespace products and customers. You'll need to recreate your site design using a Shopify theme and set up 301 redirects for SEO. Plan 1-2 weeks for a full migration. Going from Shopify to Squarespace is harder — no automated import tool exists.

Does Shopify charge transaction fees?

Not if you use Shopify Payments. You only pay the credit card processing fee (2.9% + 30¢ on Basic, decreasing on higher plans). If you use a third-party payment gateway, Shopify adds an extra 0.6-2% fee depending on your plan.

Which is better for blogging?

Squarespace, by a wide margin. Squarespace was originally a blogging platform, and its blog features — rich editor, multiple layouts, podcast support, contributor profiles, scheduling — are far superior to Shopify's basic blog. If content marketing is a major part of your strategy, Squarespace wins.

Final Verdict: Shopify vs Squarespace in 2026

Shopify and Squarespace are both excellent platforms — but they're excellent at different things.

Choose Shopify if ecommerce is your primary focus. Whether you're starting a side hustle or scaling a D2C brand, Shopify's purpose-built commerce tools, 8,000+ app ecosystem, multi-channel selling, POS system, and international capabilities make it the strongest ecommerce platform available. The $39/month starting price ($29/month annual) is a worthwhile investment when selling products is how you make money.

Choose Squarespace if your website needs to do more than just sell. Creatives, service businesses, restaurants, content creators, and businesses with a small product catalog will appreciate Squarespace's superior design tools, built-in blogging, scheduling integrations, and lower starting price. The ecommerce features are solid enough for stores that don't need Shopify's depth.

The honest answer for most people: if you're reading a comparison article trying to decide between these two, you probably know which one you need. If the word "store" comes to mind first, it's Shopify. If the word "website" comes to mind first, it's Squarespace. Trust that instinct.
