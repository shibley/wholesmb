Gusto vs Justworks (2026): Payroll Software vs PEO — Which Model Does Your Business Actually Need?

This isn't a typical software comparison. Gusto is payroll software — you run it yourself, you stay the sole employer, and you pay a transparent monthly fee starting at $49/month + $6/employee. Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) — you enter a co-employment arrangement where Justworks becomes a co-employer of your workers, handling payroll, benefits, compliance, and workers' comp for $49-$99/employee/month. We've compared both across pricing, payroll, benefits, compliance, HR support, and more with current 2026 data so you can decide which model fits.

Overview: Software vs PEO — Two Different Models

The Gusto vs Justworks comparison is fundamentally different from comparing two software platforms. You're choosing between two entirely different approaches to managing your workforce:

  • Gusto is a software tool. You buy it, you use it, you stay in the driver's seat. Think of it as hiring a very capable assistant that does exactly what you tell it to do.
  • Justworks is a service wrapped in software. You partner with them, they become a co-employer of your workers, and they handle the administrative heavy lifting. Think of it as hiring a fractional HR department.

Both companies were founded in the early 2010s to solve the same problem — small businesses drowning in payroll and HR complexity — but they arrived at opposite solutions.

Gusto said: "Let's build software so good that any founder can manage payroll and HR themselves." Founded in 2011 as ZenPayroll by Josh Reeves, Edward Kim, and Tomer London, the company has raised over $746 million in venture funding and reached an approximate valuation of $9.5 billion. As of 2026, Gusto serves over 400,000 businesses and generates approximately $735 million in annual revenue. The platform processes payroll for millions of employees across all 50 US states.

Justworks said: "Let's pool small businesses together so they can access the same infrastructure as large corporations." Founded in 2012 by Isaac Oates in New York City, Justworks has raised approximately $148-163 million in funding and serves over 12,000 customers with an estimated $350 million in annual revenue. The company is IRS-certified as a Professional Employer Organization (CPEO) — a designation requiring strict financial, bonding, and operational standards. Justworks famously withdrew its IPO in January 2022 due to market conditions, and as of late 2024 has stated it has no immediate plans to go public.

The competitive landscape reflects these different models. Gusto competes against payroll software like ADP, Paychex, OnPay, and QuickBooks Payroll. Justworks competes against other PEOs — TriNet, Insperity, ADP TotalSource, and Papaya Global — as well as newer "PEO-like" platforms like Deel (which offers PEO services starting at $125/employee/month).

PEO vs Payroll Software: What's the Actual Difference?

Before comparing features head-to-head, it's worth understanding what you're actually choosing between — because the business model differences affect everything.

How Payroll Software Works (Gusto Model)

You are the sole employer of your workers. You purchase software (Gusto) as a tool to help you run payroll, file taxes, manage benefits, and handle HR tasks. Your company's EIN goes on all tax forms. You buy your own workers' compensation policy. You shop for and select your own health insurance plans (Gusto brokers help, but you own the relationship). If something goes wrong with compliance, it's on you.

Analogy: Gusto is like buying a great set of power tools. You still build the house yourself, but the tools make the work faster and better.

How a PEO Works (Justworks Model)

You enter a co-employment arrangement. Justworks becomes the "employer of record" for administrative purposes — tax filings, workers' comp insurance, health benefits, and state registrations all run through Justworks. You remain the "worksite employer" — you hire, manage, direct, promote, and fire your employees. Justworks handles the paperwork, compliance, and insurance side.

What co-employment means in practice:

  • Your employees' W-2 forms show Justworks' EIN, not yours
  • Workers' comp insurance is under Justworks' master policy (typically cheaper due to pooling)
  • Health insurance is offered through Justworks' group plan (enterprise-level rates)
  • State tax accounts and unemployment insurance are managed by Justworks
  • You still control all hiring, firing, and management decisions

Analogy: Justworks is like hiring a general contractor. They bring their own tools, manage the permits, coordinate the subcontractors, and handle inspections. You tell them what you want the house to look like.

When Each Model Makes Sense

  • Payroll software (Gusto) works best when: you're cost-sensitive, you have straightforward compliance needs (1-2 states), you have some HR knowledge, you want full control over your employment relationships, or you're a very small team (<10 employees).
  • PEO (Justworks) works best when: you operate in multiple states, you have no HR person, you want to offer competitive benefits without a large team, you need workers' comp and EPLI without shopping for policies, or you're growing fast and need compliance infrastructure that scales.

The break-even point: Most analyses suggest a PEO starts to make financial sense around 15-25 employees — at that point, the savings on insurance premiums and the value of compliance infrastructure can offset the higher per-employee cost. Below 10 employees, payroll software is almost always more cost-effective.

Bottom line: Gusto is dramatically cheaper if you only need payroll and basic HR. Justworks' PEO model becomes competitive when you factor in workers' comp, insurance savings, compliance overhead, and the value of HR consulting — especially for businesses with 25+ employees in multiple states.

Payroll Processing & Tax Filing

Both platforms process payroll competently, but the underlying architecture differs because of the PEO model.

Gusto Payroll

Payroll is Gusto's core product — the feature that started the company and still defines it:

  • Unlimited payroll runs on every plan — regular, off-cycle, bonus, correction, commission. No extra charges.
  • AutoPilot mode: Set it and forget it for salaried employees with no variable pay. Gusto runs payroll automatically.
  • Next-day or 2-day direct deposit (4-day on Simple plan).
  • Automatic tax filing: Federal, state, and local taxes calculated, filed, and remitted. All quarterly and annual returns handled. Tax penalty protection.
  • Free state registration: Hire in a new state? Gusto registers you automatically.
  • Contractor payments: Domestic and international (80+ countries). Automatic 1099 filing.
  • Multiple pay schedules: Weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, monthly. Different schedules for different employee groups.
  • Pay-as-you-go workers' comp: Through partner AP Intego. Premiums adjust automatically based on payroll data.
  • Your company's EIN: All tax forms (W-2, 1099, 941) are filed under your company's EIN. You're the sole employer.

Justworks Payroll

Justworks' payroll is solid and reliable, though less feature-rich in terms of automation options:

  • Unlimited payroll runs — regular, off-cycle, bonus, vendor, and contractor payments.
  • Direct deposit with standard processing times.
  • Automatic tax filing: All federal and state payroll taxes calculated and filed — but under Justworks' EIN (PEO model). Your employees' W-2s show Justworks' federal tax ID.
  • State registration handled: When you hire in a new state, Justworks manages registrations under their PEO accounts — one less thing for you to worry about.
  • Contractor payments: Domestic contractors via direct deposit with automatic 1099 filing.
  • Multiple pay schedules: Support for various frequencies.
  • Workers' comp included: No separate policy needed — it's bundled into PEO plans under Justworks' master policy.

Key Payroll Differences

  • AutoPilot: Gusto has it, Justworks doesn't. For salaried teams, this is a time-saver.
  • EIN on tax forms: Gusto uses your EIN. Justworks uses theirs. This matters for some businesses — especially if you ever leave the PEO.
  • Workers' comp approach: Gusto offers pay-as-you-go through a partner (you manage the relationship). Justworks includes it as part of the PEO (they manage everything).
  • International contractors: Gusto pays in 80+ countries. Justworks pays domestic contractors only (but offers EOR for international full-time hires).
  • Deposit speed: Gusto offers next-day direct deposit on Plus/Premium. Justworks uses standard processing.

Payroll verdict: As pure payroll software, Gusto is more polished — more automation, faster deposits, international contractor support, and you maintain your own EIN. Justworks' payroll is competent but it's not the star — the compliance and benefits infrastructure around it is what you're paying for.

Justworks Benefits

Benefits are Justworks' competitive weapon — the PEO model's greatest advantage:

  • Health insurance (Plus plan): Access to enterprise-level group plans from Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Kaiser Permanente. Because Justworks pools thousands of small businesses into one large group, rates can be 10-20% lower than what you'd get independently. This is the PEO's superpower.

  • COBRA administration (Plus): Fully managed by Justworks.
  • Mental health & fertility benefits (Plus): Additional wellness perks included.
  • Commuter benefits (Basic & Plus): Pre-tax transit and parking benefits.

The Insurance Math

Here's why this matters financially. Consider a 25-employee company in New York:

  • On your own (Gusto): A small group health plan might cost $600-$800/employee/month in premiums. Workers' comp might be $150-$300/month total. You shop, compare, and manage these yourself.
  • Through Justworks (Plus): Enterprise-group rates might drop health premiums to $500-$700/employee/month — a potential savings of $100/employee/month or $2,500/month for 25 employees. Workers' comp, EPLI, and COBRA administration are included.
  • The calculus: Justworks Plus costs $2,225/month for 25 employees. If you save $2,500/month on insurance premiums, plus $200-500/month on workers' comp, the PEO could actually save you money compared to buying everything separately with Gusto.

Benefits verdict: Justworks wins decisively. Access to enterprise-level health insurance rates is the primary reason businesses choose PEOs, and Justworks delivers. If competitive benefits are critical for your talent strategy, this advantage alone can justify the higher per-employee cost.

Compliance & Risk Management

Compliance is the hidden cost of employment — and it's where the PEO vs software distinction becomes most impactful.

Gusto Compliance

  • Automatic payroll tax compliance (filing, remitting, year-end forms)
  • Free state tax registration when you hire in new states
  • Tax penalty protection (Gusto pays if they make an error)
  • Basic compliance alerts on Premium plan
  • Certified HR experts available on Premium plan for compliance questions
  • HR resource center with templates, guides, and state-specific information
  • New hire reporting automatically submitted to state agencies
  • You are responsible for: Understanding state labor laws, maintaining employment practices, managing workers' comp claims, handling COBRA notifications, and ensuring employment law compliance across all states you operate in

Justworks Compliance

  • All payroll tax compliance handled under Justworks' accounts
  • State unemployment insurance (SUI) managed by Justworks — no need to register or manage your own SUI accounts
  • Workers' comp insurance and claims management included
  • Employment practices liability insurance (EPLI) included
  • Mandatory harassment prevention training provided (critical for NY, CA, IL, CT, DE, ME employers)
  • Certified HR consultants available for compliance guidance
  • ACA compliance support (tracking hours, filing 1095-C forms)
  • COBRA administration (Plus plan) — notices, elections, and premium collection handled
  • State labor law poster compliance updates
  • New hire reporting across all states
  • Justworks assumes shared responsibility for: Tax filings, workers' comp, unemployment insurance, and benefits compliance. This significantly reduces your legal exposure.

The Compliance Reality

For a single-state business with 10 employees, compliance is manageable with Gusto. But consider a business with 25 employees across 5 states:

  • 5 different state tax registrations to manage
  • 5 different unemployment insurance accounts
  • Multiple state-specific labor law requirements (paid leave, minimum wage, harassment training)
  • Workers' comp policy that covers multiple states
  • ACA tracking and reporting
  • State-specific COBRA or mini-COBRA requirements

With Gusto, you (or an HR consultant you hire) manage all of this. With Justworks, it's handled as part of the service.

Compliance verdict: Justworks wins significantly — especially for multi-state businesses. The PEO model was specifically designed to solve compliance complexity, and Justworks' IRS CPEO certification provides an additional layer of financial and operational accountability.

FAQ: Gusto vs Justworks

What is the main difference between Gusto and Justworks?

The fundamental difference is the business model. Gusto is payroll software — you manage your own payroll, HR, and compliance using Gusto's tools, and you remain the sole employer of your workers. Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) — you enter a co-employment arrangement where Justworks becomes a co-employer of your workers, handling payroll, benefits, compliance, and workers' compensation on your behalf. Think of it as DIY-with-great-tools (Gusto) vs outsourced-to-experts (Justworks). Gusto costs less ($49/month + $6/employee) but requires more self-management. Justworks costs more ($49-$99/employee) but takes more off your plate.

How much does Gusto cost compared to Justworks in 2026?

Gusto Simple: $49/month + $6/employee. Gusto Plus: $80/month + $12/employee. Justworks Payroll-only: $50/month + $8/employee. Justworks PEO Basic: $49/employee/month (under 25 employees). Justworks PEO Plus: $99/employee/month. For a 25-employee company, Gusto Simple costs $199/month while Justworks Basic costs $1,100/month — a difference of $901/month. However, Justworks includes workers' comp insurance, compliance support, EPLI, and HR consulting in that price. When you add those separately to Gusto, the gap narrows significantly.

Is a PEO better than payroll software for small businesses?

It depends on your size and needs. Most businesses with under 10 employees are better served by payroll software (much cheaper, compliance is simpler). Between 15-25 employees with multi-state operations, a PEO starts to make more sense — the compliance burden grows faster than most founders expect, and insurance pooling can offset the higher cost. Above 50 employees in 5+ states, PEOs often save money when you factor in the cost of workers' comp, EPLI, compliance consulting, and benefits administration that you'd otherwise buy separately.

What does co-employment mean with Justworks?

Co-employment means Justworks becomes the "employer of record" for tax and benefits purposes. Your employees' W-2s show Justworks' EIN, workers' comp is under Justworks' master policy, and state registrations run through their accounts. You remain the "worksite employer" — you still hire, fire, manage, and direct your employees. Justworks handles the administrative side. The benefits include access to better insurance rates, simplified compliance, and reduced liability. The drawbacks are that some employees may be confused by the arrangement, and leaving the PEO requires transitioning all these functions back to your company.

Can I switch from Justworks to Gusto (or vice versa)?

Yes, but switching from a PEO (Justworks) to payroll software (Gusto) is more complex. You'll need to register your own state tax and unemployment accounts, secure your own workers' comp policy, transition health insurance, and update your EIN since Justworks' EIN was on your W-2s. Plan for 30-60 days. Going from Gusto to Justworks is simpler — Justworks handles most of the transition during onboarding. Both allow month-to-month billing with no long-term contracts.

Does Justworks offer a payroll-only plan without PEO?

Yes — Justworks launched a standalone Payroll plan at $50/month base + $8/employee/month. It includes payroll, direct deposit, tax filings, and basic onboarding without the co-employment arrangement. However, it's only available in select states (not all 50). Gusto Simple ($49/month + $6/employee) offers similar features with broader state coverage and more integrations, making it the stronger pure payroll option.

Which has better health insurance options?

Justworks typically offers better rates because the PEO pools thousands of employees, giving access to enterprise-level plans from Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, and Kaiser Permanente. This can mean 10-20% lower premiums. Gusto is a licensed broker that helps you find plans, but your rates are based on your company alone. For very small businesses (under 10 employees), the insurance savings from Justworks can sometimes offset the higher per-employee cost. Note: health insurance is only on Justworks Plus ($99/employee), not Basic ($49/employee).

Is Justworks or Gusto better for remote and multi-state teams?

Both handle multi-state payroll, but Justworks has a clear edge for multi-state compliance. As a PEO, Justworks automatically manages state registrations, unemployment insurance, workers' comp coverage, and state labor law compliance. Gusto also supports multi-state payroll (all 50 states) with free state tax registration, but you're more responsible for understanding employment law in each state. For fully remote teams with employees in 5+ states, Justworks' comprehensive compliance coverage is worth the premium. For international hiring, Justworks offers EOR services; Gusto only pays international contractors.

The Bottom Line

Gusto and Justworks aren't really competitors — they're different answers to the same question. Gusto says "here are the best tools — you've got this." Justworks says "hand it over — we've got this."

For most businesses starting out, Gusto is the right first choice: it's affordable, intuitive, and includes everything you need to run payroll and basic HR. As your business grows past 25 employees, expands to multiple states, and starts needing competitive benefits to attract talent, the PEO model becomes increasingly attractive — and Justworks is one of the best in the space.

The worst choice is the one you don't make. Either platform is infinitely better than spreadsheets, manual tax filings, and crossed fingers.
