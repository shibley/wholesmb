Gusto vs Justworks (2026): Payroll Platform vs PEO — Which Model Does Your Business Actually Need?

Gusto and Justworks solve the same core problem — paying and managing your employees — but through fundamentally different business models. Gusto is a standalone payroll platform: you buy software, you run payroll, you remain the sole employer. Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO): you enter a co-employment relationship, pool your employees with thousands of other small businesses, and get enterprise-grade benefits and compliance in return. We've compared both across pricing, payroll, benefits, compliance, HR tools, and more with current 2026 data.

Both are legitimate approaches — the right one depends on what keeps you up at night.

Overview: Platform vs PEO — Two Different Philosophies

The Gusto vs Justworks comparison isn't really about feature checklists — it's about fundamentally different business models for solving the same problem.

Gusto sells software. Founded in 2011 as ZenPayroll, Gusto built a payroll-first platform designed to make paying people simple and transparent. Over the years, Gusto expanded into benefits brokerage, HR tools, hiring, time tracking, and compliance — becoming a comprehensive HR platform. But the relationship is straightforward: you buy software, you run it, your employees work for you and only you. As of 2026, Gusto serves over 300,000 businesses, processes payroll for more than 6 million employees across all 50 US states, and has raised over $746 million in funding at an approximate $9.5 billion valuation.

Justworks sells a partnership. Founded in 2012, Justworks is a certified Professional Employer Organization (PEO) that enters a co-employment relationship with your business. Your employees become jointly employed by both your company and Justworks — which lets Justworks pool thousands of small businesses together to negotiate enterprise-level health insurance, bundle workers' compensation, and provide compliance infrastructure that no small business could build alone. As of 2026, Justworks is IRS-certified, ESAC-accredited, has approximately 1,500 employees, processed over $31 billion in payroll in 2025, and serves primarily small to mid-size businesses with 2-100+ employees. The company has raised approximately $243 million in funding.

These different models create meaningfully different value propositions:

  • Gusto excels at payroll processing, transparent pricing, benefits brokerage, financial tools for employees (Gusto Wallet), extensive integrations (190+ apps), and giving you complete control over the employer relationship.
  • Justworks excels at enterprise health insurance access, bundled workers' comp and EPLI, compliance peace-of-mind, 24/7 HR support, and removing the administrative burden of being a multi-state employer.

The competitive landscape reflects this difference. Gusto competes against payroll platforms — ADP, Paychex, and OnPay — on features and price. Justworks competes against other PEOs — ADP TotalSource, TriNet, and Insperity — on benefits quality, service level, and ease of the co-employment experience.

Notably, Justworks recognized that not everyone wants a PEO and launched a standalone Payroll plan at $50/month + $8/employee — directly competing with Gusto on its home turf. This gives Justworks a foot in both worlds, though the Payroll plan is newer and less mature than Gusto's decade-old payroll engine.

PEO vs Payroll Software: What's the Actual Difference?

Before comparing features, it's important to understand what a PEO actually is — because this distinction drives everything else in this comparison.

The Payroll Software Model (Gusto)

When you use Gusto, the relationship is simple:

  • You buy software to help you process payroll, file taxes, and manage HR tasks
  • You are the sole employer of your workers
  • You select and manage your own health insurance plans (Gusto acts as broker)
  • You maintain your own workers' compensation policy
  • You handle compliance obligations (with software assistance)
  • You can cancel anytime — no co-employment to unwind

Think of it like buying a car. You own it, you drive it, you're responsible for maintenance and insurance. Gusto is the car — it gets you from point A to point B, and you're in complete control.

The PEO Model (Justworks)

When you use Justworks PEO, the relationship is more complex:

  • You enter a co-employment agreement — your employees become jointly employed by your company AND Justworks
  • Justworks becomes the "employer of record" for tax and benefits purposes
  • Your employees appear on Justworks' tax filings (EIN), not yours
  • Justworks pools your employees with thousands of others to negotiate enterprise health insurance rates
  • Workers' comp, EPLI, and unemployment insurance are bundled under Justworks' master policies
  • You retain control over day-to-day management — hiring, firing, scheduling, directing work
  • Leaving the PEO is a more involved transition than canceling software

Think of it like joining a co-op. You give up some autonomy in exchange for group buying power and shared infrastructure. Justworks is the co-op — you benefit from collective scale, but you're in a partnership, not flying solo.

Why This Matters for Your Business

The PEO model creates real advantages for certain businesses:

  • Benefits access: A 15-person company can offer the same quality health plans as a 5,000-person company
  • Compliance delegation: The PEO assumes liability for payroll tax compliance and helps manage employment law obligations
  • Bundled coverage: Workers' comp + EPLI + unemployment insurance are included, not separate purchases
  • Administrative offloading: The PEO handles more of the operational burden

But it also creates real tradeoffs:

  • Higher per-employee cost: You're paying for the partnership, not just the software
  • Less flexibility: You work within the PEO's benefits options, not the open market
  • Switching costs: Leaving a PEO is harder than canceling a SaaS subscription
  • Shared employer relationship: Some businesses are uncomfortable with co-employment
  • Less control over benefits: You choose from the PEO's menu, not the entire market

Bottom line: If you're a 5-person company that just needs to run payroll, a PEO is overkill. If you're a 30-person company struggling to offer competitive health insurance and terrified of compliance missteps, a PEO might be exactly what you need.

Pricing Compared: Per-Employee Economics

Pricing is where the payroll-vs-PEO distinction becomes most concrete — and where many businesses make their decision.

Gusto: Low Base + Per Employee

Gusto uses a base fee + per-employee model with everything included in each tier:

  • Simple: $49 + ($6 × employees)
  • Plus: $80 + ($12 × employees)
  • Premium: ~$180 + (~$22 × employees)

For a 25-employee company:

  • Simple: $49 + $150 = $199/month ($7.96/employee effective)
  • Plus: $80 + $300 = $380/month ($15.20/employee effective)
  • Premium: ~$180 + $550 = ~$730/month (~$29.20/employee effective)

Justworks: Flat Per-Employee

Justworks uses a simpler per-employee model (PEO plans have no base fee):

  • Payroll: $50 + ($8 × employees)
  • PEO Basic: $59 × employees
  • PEO Plus: $109 × employees

For a 25-employee company:

  • Payroll: $50 + $200 = $250/month ($10/employee effective)
  • PEO Basic: 25 × $59 = $1,475/month
  • PEO Plus: 25 × $109 = $2,725/month

The Real Cost Comparison

Comparing Gusto to Justworks PEO on sticker price alone is misleading. Here's why:

  • Payroll-only: Gusto Simple ($199/mo) vs Justworks Payroll ($250/mo) — Gusto wins by ~20% and has a more mature payroll product with 190+ integrations.
  • Full platform: Gusto Plus ($380/mo) vs Justworks PEO Basic ($1,475/mo) — Justworks is ~3.9x more expensive, but includes workers' comp, EPLI, and 24/7 HR consulting that Gusto doesn't bundle.
  • Full platform + health insurance: Gusto Plus ($380/mo) vs Justworks PEO Plus ($2,725/mo) — Justworks is ~7x more expensive on the platform fee. But if Justworks saves $40-50/employee/month on health insurance premiums through group buying power, the gap narrows significantly.

The Hidden Cost Math

To make a fair comparison, you need to add the costs that Gusto charges separately (or doesn't offer) that Justworks bundles:

  • Workers' compensation: Gusto offers pay-as-you-go workers' comp, but it's an additional cost based on your industry risk and payroll. For a 25-person office business, expect $100-$300+/month. Bundled in Justworks PEO.
  • EPLI coverage: Gusto doesn't offer this. Purchased separately, EPLI typically costs $1,000-$5,000+/year ($83-$417+/month) depending on company size and industry. Bundled in Justworks PEO.
  • HR consulting: Gusto offers certified HR experts on Premium only. Justworks includes HR support on all PEO plans. Independent HR consulting costs $100-$300/hour. Bundled in Justworks PEO.
  • Health insurance premiums: This is the biggest variable. If Justworks' group buying power saves $30-50/employee/month on health premiums compared to what you'd get as a small business through Gusto's brokerage, that's $750-$1,250/month saved for 25 employees.

Bottom line: For payroll-only, Gusto is clearly cheaper. For the full package including benefits, compliance, and risk management, the gap between Gusto and Justworks shrinks dramatically when you factor in everything you'd need to buy separately. For some businesses, Justworks PEO may actually be cheaper than Gusto once health insurance savings are counted.

Payroll Processing & Tax Filing

Both platforms handle payroll well, but Gusto has a decade-long head start in payroll-specific refinement.

Gusto Payroll

Payroll is Gusto's core product, and it shows:

  • Unlimited payroll runs on every plan — off-cycle, bonus, correction, commission. No extra charges.
  • AutoPilot mode: For salaried employees with no variable pay, payroll runs completely automatically. Set it and forget it.
  • Next-day or 2-day direct deposit — fast access to funds without waiting.
  • Automatic tax filing: Federal, state, and local taxes calculated, filed, and remitted. Quarterly returns, W-2s, W-3s, 1099s — all handled. Tax penalty protection if Gusto makes an error.
  • Free state registration: Hire in a new state? Gusto handles the tax registration at no cost.
  • Pay-as-you-go workers' comp: Integrated with payroll so premiums adjust automatically — no large upfront deposit.
  • Contractor payments: Domestic and international (80+ countries) with automatic 1099 filing.
  • Gusto Wallet: Employees get early wage access (up to $1,000), fee-free savings accounts, and budgeting tools.

Gusto's payroll interface is designed for non-accountants. It walks you through each step, flags anomalies, and provides a clear summary before approval. Most users spend less than 10 minutes per payroll run.

Justworks Payroll

Justworks processes payroll effectively — the company handled $31 billion in payroll in 2025. But payroll was originally part of the PEO bundle, not a standalone product:

  • Unlimited payroll runs with direct deposit — including off-cycle and bonus payments.
  • Automatic tax calculations and filing across 4,000+ tax localities in all 50 states.
  • Year-end tax documents: W-2 and 1099 preparation and filing.
  • Multiple pay schedules and job code support for complex compensation structures.
  • Contractor payments: Domestic contractors and international (40+ countries for EOR).
  • Clean interface: Users consistently praise the platform's simplicity and ease of use.

Justworks' payroll is competent and handles the essentials well, but lacks some of Gusto's polished features — no AutoPilot mode, no early wage access for employees, and significantly fewer integrations. The standalone Payroll plan is newer and may lack the refinement of Gusto's decade-old engine.

Payroll Verdict

Gusto wins on payroll refinement and features. Both process payroll reliably, but Gusto's AutoPilot mode, Gusto Wallet, extensive integrations (190+ vs ~8), and years of payroll-focused iteration make it the stronger payroll product. If payroll processing is your primary concern and you don't need PEO services, Gusto is the clear choice. However, Justworks handles payroll perfectly well for businesses that choose it for the PEO benefits.

Benefits & Health Insurance

Justworks Benefits (PEO Plus)

Through co-employment, Justworks pools all of its client companies' employees into a single large group for benefits purposes. This unlocks:

  • Enterprise-grade health insurance: Medical, dental, and vision plans from national carriers (Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, Kaiser, and others) at rates typically 10-30% lower than what a small business can access independently. A 15-person company gets the same bargaining power as a 15,000-person company.
  • Health Savings Accounts (HSA) and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA): Tax-advantaged savings for medical expenses, with easy enrollment and payroll deduction integration.
  • Mental health benefits: Access to mental health coverage and Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs) — increasingly important for talent retention.
  • 401(k) retirement plans: Included in all PEO plans. Employees can save for retirement with optional employer matching.
  • Life and disability insurance: Group rates for life, short-term, and long-term disability coverage.
  • Pre-tax commuter benefits: Transit and parking savings deducted pre-tax from employee paychecks.
  • Fitness memberships: Discounted gym and wellness programs — a popular perk for employee retention.
  • Workers' compensation (PEO Basic/Plus): Bundled into the PEO fee — no separate policy, no upfront deposit, no annual audit surprises.
  • EPLI (PEO Basic/Plus): Employment Practices Liability Insurance covers claims of wrongful termination, discrimination, and harassment. This alone would cost $1,000-$5,000+/year if purchased independently.

Gusto Benefits

Gusto acts as a licensed insurance broker — it helps you find and enroll in benefits, but you're buying as an individual small business:

  • Health insurance brokerage: Gusto is a licensed broker in all 50 states. It helps you compare and select health, dental, and vision plans from multiple carriers. However, you're purchasing as your company — your rates are based on your company size and employee demographics.
  • 401(k): Through partnership with Guideline — easy setup, competitive fees, automatic payroll deduction.
  • HSAs, FSAs, commuter benefits: Available across all plans.

  • 529 college savings: Unique to Gusto — employees can save for education with payroll deductions.
  • Gusto Wallet: Financial wellness app with early wage access, fee-free savings accounts, and budgeting tools — a benefit that Justworks doesn't match.

The key limitation: Gusto doesn't include EPLI, and its workers' comp is an add-on rather than bundled. More importantly, because you're buying insurance as a small business (not through a pooled group), your health insurance rates will typically be higher than what Justworks can negotiate through its PEO.

Benefits Verdict

Justworks wins on benefits access and cost — significantly. The PEO co-employment model is specifically designed to give small businesses enterprise-level benefits purchasing power. If offering competitive health insurance is critical to your ability to hire and retain talent, Justworks' structural advantage is hard to replicate. Gusto offers adequate benefits administration and brokerage, plus unique perks like Gusto Wallet and 529 savings, but can't match the group purchasing power of a PEO. For businesses where benefits quality is a top-3 priority, this section alone may decide the comparison.

Compliance & Risk Management

Compliance is one of the most underappreciated differences between a payroll platform and a PEO.

Justworks Compliance

As a PEO, Justworks assumes shared responsibility for compliance — it's not just software helping you, it's a partner with skin in the game:

  • Multi-state employer compliance: Justworks supports 4,000+ tax localities and automatically handles state registration, unemployment insurance, and labor law requirements when you hire in new states.
  • EPLI coverage: Built-in protection against employment-related lawsuits — wrongful termination, discrimination, harassment claims. This is insurance you'd need to buy separately without a PEO.
  • Workers' comp management: Bundled coverage with no annual audit surprises. Justworks handles the policy, the premiums, and the claims process.
  • IRS certification: Justworks is IRS-certified as a Certified PEO (CPEO), meaning the IRS has verified its financial responsibility and tax compliance. This certification also means your business gets additional tax protections.
  • ESAC accreditation: The Employer Services Assurance Corporation has accredited Justworks for financial reliability, ethical business practices, and operational standards.
  • HR consulting: Access to HR professionals who can answer compliance questions, review employee handbooks, and help navigate tricky employment situations — included in all PEO plans.
  • Regulatory monitoring: Justworks tracks labor law changes and updates its platform accordingly, reducing your risk of falling behind on new requirements.

Gusto Compliance

Gusto provides compliance tools and guidance, but the ultimate responsibility stays with you:

  • Automatic tax filing: Gusto handles payroll tax calculations, filings, and remittances — this is compliance automation, not compliance delegation.
  • Tax penalty protection: If Gusto makes a payroll tax error, they pay the penalty. However, compliance responsibilities beyond payroll tax (labor law, handbook policies, termination procedures) remain yours.
  • Free state registration: When you hire in a new state, Gusto handles the employer tax registration.
  • Compliance alerts (Premium): Notifications about regulatory changes, required postings, and compliance deadlines — but only on the Premium plan.
  • Certified HR experts (Premium): Access to HR professionals for compliance guidance, but again, only at the Premium tier.
  • HR resource center (Premium): Templates, guides, and compliance checklists. Helpful but self-serve.

Compliance Verdict

Justworks wins decisively on compliance. The PEO model provides a fundamentally different level of protection — bundled EPLI, IRS certification, shared compliance responsibility, and HR consulting included in every PEO plan. With Gusto, you get excellent payroll tax compliance automation, but labor law compliance, employment practices liability, and many other risk areas remain your responsibility. For businesses operating in multiple states, in litigious industries, or without in-house HR expertise, Justworks' compliance infrastructure is a significant advantage.

  • Platform + extras: ~$460-$660/mo
  • Year 1 Total (excl. health premiums): ~$5,520-$7,920

Justworks PEO Plus

  • Platform: 15 × $109 = $1,635/mo
  • Workers' comp: Included
  • EPLI: Included
  • Health insurance premiums: At enterprise group rates (10-30% lower)
  • Platform: $1,635/mo
  • Year 1 Total (excl. health premiums): $19,620

On platform costs alone, Gusto is $12,000-$14,000 cheaper per year for 15 employees. However, if Justworks saves $40-$50/employee/month on health insurance through group rates, that's $600-$750/month savings = $7,200-$9,000/year. This narrows the gap to $3,000-$7,000/year — and the remaining difference buys you 24/7 HR consulting, shared compliance responsibility, and peace of mind that some businesses value highly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gusto or Justworks better for small businesses?

Gusto is generally better for small businesses primarily needing payroll — starting at $49/month + $6/employee with transparent pricing and everything included. Justworks is better for small businesses that need enterprise health insurance, bundled compliance, and an HR partner — but at $59/employee/month (PEO Basic), it costs more per employee. For businesses under 15 employees, Gusto is almost always more cost-effective. For businesses with 15-100+ employees where benefits quality matters for hiring, Justworks can justify its premium.

What is a PEO and do I need one?

A PEO (Professional Employer Organization) enters a co-employment relationship with your business. Your employees become jointly employed by you and the PEO, which lets the PEO pool thousands of small businesses together for enterprise health insurance rates and bundled compliance. You need a PEO if: (1) you can't offer competitive health insurance as a small business, (2) you're terrified of multi-state compliance, (3) you don't have in-house HR, or (4) you want workers' comp and EPLI bundled. You don't need a PEO if you just need payroll software and basic HR tools.

Does Justworks have a payroll-only plan?

Yes — Justworks offers a standalone Payroll plan at $50/month base + $8/employee. This includes payroll processing, tax filing, time tracking, and basic HR tools without the PEO co-employment model. However, it doesn't include enterprise health insurance, workers' comp, or EPLI. For payroll-only, Gusto Simple ($49 + $6/employee) is slightly cheaper and has a more mature payroll product with 190+ integrations vs Justworks' ~8.

How much can Justworks save on health insurance?

Justworks' PEO model typically provides health insurance premiums that are 10-30% lower than what a small business can negotiate independently. For a 25-employee company, if the average savings is $40-50/employee/month, that's $12,000-$15,000 per year in premium savings. The actual savings depend on your company's size, employee demographics, location, and current health plan. Request quotes from both Gusto (as a broker) and Justworks to compare specific numbers for your business.

Can I switch from Justworks to Gusto (or vice versa)?

Switching from Gusto to Justworks is relatively straightforward — Justworks will help transition your payroll data and set up the PEO relationship. Switching from Justworks PEO to Gusto is more complex because you need to unwind the co-employment relationship: re-establish your own employer accounts, find your own health insurance plans (employees may lose current coverage at renewal), set up your own workers' comp policy, and transfer all payroll data. Plan for a 30-60 day transition aligned with your benefits renewal date.

Is Justworks worth the higher cost?

Justworks is worth it if: (1) health insurance savings through group buying power narrow or eliminate the cost gap, (2) you'd need to purchase EPLI, workers' comp, and HR consulting separately with Gusto, and (3) you value 24/7 HR support and shared compliance responsibility. It's not worth it if: you primarily need payroll, have fewer than 10-15 employees, or your industry/location means health insurance savings are minimal. Do the total cost math for your specific situation before deciding.

Which has better integrations?

Gusto — by a wide margin. Gusto has 190+ integrations covering accounting (QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks), time tracking (TSheets, Homebase), expense management (Expensify), project management (Asana, Monday.com), and more. Justworks has about 8 direct integrations (primarily QuickBooks and Xero). If your business runs on a stack of interconnected tools, Gusto fits in better. If you're looking for an all-in-one solution that handles most things internally, Justworks' limited integrations may not matter.

Can Gusto or Justworks handle international employees?

Both have international capabilities. Gusto pays international contractors in 80+ countries and offers EOR services in 9 countries through Remote ($599/employee/month). Justworks offers EOR in 100+ countries ($599/employee/month) and contractor payments in 40+ countries. Neither is a dedicated global payroll platform — for extensive international hiring, consider Deel, Remote, or Oyster instead.
