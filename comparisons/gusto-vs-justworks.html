Gusto vs Justworks (2026): Self-Service Payroll vs Full-Service PEO — Which Model Actually Makes Sense for Your Business?

Gusto and Justworks represent two fundamentally different approaches to managing your workforce. Gusto is a self-service payroll and HR platform — you pay a transparent monthly fee, manage payroll yourself with software, and maintain full control over benefits and compliance. Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) — you pay a higher fee, they become your co-employer, and they handle everything (payroll, benefits, compliance, HR) as a white-glove service. We've compared both across pricing, benefits access, compliance, HR depth, and real-world cost scenarios with current 2026 data.

Overview: PEO vs Payroll Platform

The Gusto vs Justworks comparison is fundamentally a comparison between two different business models: self-service payroll software (Gusto) vs full-service PEO (Justworks).

Gusto is a technology platform. You sign up, connect your bank account, add your employees, and run payroll yourself — all through software. Gusto automates tax filings, offers benefits brokerage, and provides HR tools, but you remain the employer of record. You're in control. You're also responsible for staying compliant, choosing benefits carriers, and managing the details. Gusto makes this easier with software and support, but ultimately you're the one clicking "Run Payroll" every pay period.

Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO). When you sign up, Justworks becomes your co-employer — your employees are legally employed by both your company and Justworks simultaneously. Justworks handles payroll, taxes, benefits enrollment, workers' comp, compliance filings, and HR administration on your behalf. You focus on running your business; Justworks handles the people infrastructure. The trade-off is significantly higher cost and less control over vendor selection and processes.

Both approaches are valid. The question is: do you want to manage payroll yourself with software, or do you want someone else to manage it for you?

The PEO Model Explained (Co-Employment)

Understanding the co-employment model is critical to evaluating Justworks vs Gusto.

What Is Co-Employment?

When you partner with a PEO like Justworks, your employees become employees of both your company and Justworks simultaneously:

  • You (the client company) retain control over day-to-day work, hiring, firing, promotions, and business operations.
  • Justworks (the PEO) becomes the employer of record for tax purposes, handles payroll tax filings, provides workers' comp coverage, and administers benefits.

Your employees receive paychecks from Justworks, appear on Justworks' tax filings, and are covered under Justworks' workers' comp and benefits policies. But they still work for you — you manage their work, set their schedule, and decide whether to keep or terminate them.

Benefits of Co-Employment

  • Access to Fortune 500-level benefits: Because Justworks pools thousands of workers across hundreds of companies, it can negotiate with health insurance carriers (Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare) for large-group rates that small businesses cannot access independently. This often results in 10-30% lower premiums.
  • Simplified compliance: Justworks handles multi-state payroll tax filings, new hire reporting, unemployment claims, workers' comp renewals, and regulatory changes. You don't need to track 50 different state laws.
  • Lower workers' comp costs: PEOs pool risk across many companies, which can result in lower workers' comp premiums than you'd get shopping individually — especially for high-risk industries like construction or manufacturing.
  • HR expertise on demand: Justworks provides HR advisors who can help with employment law questions, harassment investigations, terminations, FMLA, ADA accommodations, and policy development.
  • Reduced admin burden: Payroll, benefits enrollment, open enrollment, tax filings, and compliance paperwork are all handled by Justworks. You save time.

Drawbacks of Co-Employment

  • Higher cost: As shown above, PEO services cost 7-10x more than self-service payroll software like Gusto.
  • Less control: You're limited to the benefits carriers, 401(k) providers, and workers' comp policies that Justworks negotiates. You can't shop around or switch carriers mid-year.
  • Harder to leave: Exiting a PEO requires unwinding the co-employment relationship, which can take 30-90 days and is best timed to coincide with benefits renewal dates (usually January 1). You'll also lose access to PEO pooling rates and need to renegotiate health insurance independently.
  • Less transparency: Because Justworks handles payroll tax filings on your behalf, you have less visibility into exactly what's being filed and when. Most PEOs provide reports, but you're trusting them to handle compliance correctly.
  • Vendor lock-in: Once your employees are on Justworks' health plans and 401(k), switching to a different provider mid-year can be disruptive and expensive.

Gusto Is Not a PEO

Gusto does not use a co-employment model. When you use Gusto:

  • You remain the employer of record. Your employees are your employees, not Gusto's.
  • You control benefits selection. Gusto acts as a benefits broker and helps you shop health plans, but you choose the carrier and plan design.
  • You manage compliance. Gusto automates tax filings and provides compliance tools, but you're ultimately responsible for staying compliant.
  • You have full flexibility. You can switch benefits carriers, change payroll providers, or cancel Gusto at any time without unwinding a co-employment relationship.

The trade-off: you don't get access to PEO pooling rates, and you have to manage more of the details yourself (though Gusto makes this easier with software and support).

Payroll Processing & Tax Filing

Both platforms handle payroll processing and tax filing, but the experience is different.

Gusto: Self-Service Payroll (5-10 Minutes Per Pay Period)

With Gusto, you run payroll every pay period. The process takes 5-10 minutes:

  1. Gusto reminds you when payroll is due (via email and in-app notification)
  2. You review hours worked (if using time tracking) or confirm salaried employee pay
  3. Gusto calculates taxes, deductions, and net pay automatically
  4. You review the payroll summary and click "Approve"
  5. Gusto debits your bank account and delivers direct deposits to employees (standard 2-day delivery, or next-day on Plus/Premium plans)
  6. Gusto files federal, state, and local payroll taxes automatically and remits payments on your behalf
  7. Employees receive pay stubs via the Gusto app or email

You can run payroll on any schedule — weekly, biweekly, monthly, or custom (e.g., semimonthly on the 15th and last day of the month). There's no limit to the number of payroll runs.

Tax filing: Gusto files and remits all federal payroll taxes (FICA, federal income tax withholding, FUTA), state unemployment taxes (SUTA), and local taxes (where applicable). At year-end, Gusto automatically generates and e-files W-2s for employees and 1099s for contractors. You don't need to do anything — Gusto handles it.

Multi-state payroll: Available on Plus and Premium plans. Gusto automatically calculates state and local taxes for employees working in different states and files taxes in each jurisdiction.

Justworks: White-Glove Payroll (Hands-Off)

With Justworks, payroll is handled for you. You don't "run payroll" — Justworks does:

  1. You submit hours worked or approve salaried pay via the Justworks platform
  2. Justworks calculates taxes, deductions, and net pay
  3. Justworks processes payroll, delivers direct deposits, and files all payroll taxes on your behalf
  4. Employees receive pay stubs via the Justworks app

Because Justworks is your co-employer, Justworks is the employer of record for tax purposes. Your employees' W-2s show Justworks as the employer, and Justworks remits all payroll taxes under its own tax ID numbers. This simplifies multi-state compliance — Justworks handles state registrations, tax filings, and unemployment claims across all 50 states without you needing to track different jurisdictions.

The trade-off: You have less visibility into the payroll process. Justworks provides reports and summaries, but you're trusting them to handle everything correctly. Most businesses find this acceptable in exchange for not having to think about payroll at all.

Winner: Tie (Different Approaches)

If you want control and transparency, Gusto wins. If you want hands-off service, Justworks wins. Both platforms handle payroll accurately and file taxes on time — the difference is whether you want to click "Approve Payroll" yourself or have someone else do it for you.

Benefits Administration & Access

This is where Justworks pulls ahead — access to Fortune 500-level benefits is the primary selling point of the PEO model.

Gusto: Benefits Brokerage (You Choose Carriers)

Gusto acts as a benefits broker. The platform helps you:

  • Shop health, dental, and vision plans from major carriers (Aetna, Blue Cross Blue Shield, Cigna, Kaiser, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, and more)
  • Compare plans side-by-side and see employee costs
  • Enroll employees and manage open enrollment digitally
  • Administer 401(k) plans through Guideline or Vestwell (Gusto partners)
  • Offer FSA, HSA, commuter benefits, and supplemental insurance (life, disability, critical illness)

Gusto partners with SimplyInsured for health insurance shopping. You're purchasing small-group plans directly from carriers under your own company name. This means:

  • You have full control over which carrier and plan you choose
  • You can switch carriers at renewal (typically annually)
  • You pay market rates for small-group coverage (typically higher than large-group rates)

Gusto does not offer self-funded plans or PEO pooling — you're shopping the small-group market like any other small business.

Justworks: PEO Pooling (Fortune 500-Level Benefits)

Justworks offers master health plans negotiated with major carriers (Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Kaiser, Oxford). Because Justworks pools thousands of workers across hundreds of companies, it can negotiate large-group rates that small businesses cannot access independently.

Benefits available through Justworks include:

  • Health, dental, and vision insurance — National and regional plans with multiple tier options (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum). Premiums are often 10-30% lower than small-group market rates.
  • 401(k) with company match — Administered through Vanguard. Justworks Plus includes up to 4% company match (a benefit that typically costs $2,000-$4,000 per employee per year).
  • Life and disability insurance — Group term life, short-term disability (STD), and long-term disability (LTD) at group rates.
  • Mental health benefits — Access to Spring Health and Talkspace for therapy and counseling.
  • Fertility benefits — Carrot Fertility and Maven for IVF, adoption, surrogacy, and family planning.
  • Commuter benefits — Pre-tax transit and parking (up to IRS limits of $315/month in 2026).
  • FSA and HSA — Flexible spending accounts and health savings accounts.
  • Student loan repayment assistance — Employer contributions toward employee student loans (available on Plus plan).

The quality and breadth of benefits through Justworks is significantly stronger than what most small businesses can negotiate independently. However, you're limited to the carriers and plan options that Justworks offers — you can't shop around or bring your own carrier.

Winner: Justworks (If You Value Premium Benefits)

If benefits are a top priority — especially health insurance with lower premiums, 401(k) matching, and perks like fertility benefits and mental health support — Justworks delivers more value. If you want flexibility to choose your own carriers and don't need enterprise-grade benefits, Gusto gives you more control at lower cost.

Health Insurance (PEO Pooling vs Small-Group)

This is the single biggest financial advantage of the PEO model.

Gusto: Small-Group Market Rates

When you shop health insurance through Gusto, you're purchasing small-group plans on the ACA market. Premiums are based on:

  • Your company's size (1-50 employees in most states)
  • Employee demographics (age, location, tobacco use)
  • Plan design (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum metal tiers)

Small-group premiums tend to be higher than large-group rates because the risk pool is smaller and carriers have less negotiating leverage. For a typical small business with 25 employees:

  • Employee-only coverage: $400-$700/month
  • Family coverage: $1,200-$2,000/month

Gusto helps you compare plans and enroll employees, but it can't change the underlying market rates.

Justworks: Large-Group PEO Rates

Justworks offers master health plans negotiated with major carriers at large-group rates. Because Justworks pools tens of thousands of workers across its entire client base, it has significantly more negotiating leverage with carriers than a 10-employee company shopping independently.

The result: premiums are often 10-30% lower than small-group market rates. For the same 25-employee company:

  • Employee-only coverage: $350-$600/month (10-20% cheaper)
  • Family coverage: $1,000-$1,700/month (15-25% cheaper)

Over a year, these savings can add up to $50,000-$150,000 for a 25-employee company — enough to offset a significant portion of the PEO fees.

Real-World Example

A 25-employee tech startup in San Francisco comparing Gusto vs Justworks:

  • Gusto: Small-group Gold PPO plan costs $14,000/month in total premiums ($8,000 employer + $6,000 employee share). Annual cost: $168,000.
  • Justworks: PEO pooling Gold PPO plan costs $11,500/month in total premiums ($6,500 employer + $5,000 employee share). Annual cost: $138,000.
  • Savings: $30,000/year in health insurance premiums.

If Justworks charges $3,000/month in PEO fees ($36,000/year), the net cost increase is only $6,000/year after accounting for health insurance savings. The company also gets 401(k) matching, fertility benefits, mental health support, and HR advisory services for that incremental $6,000 — making the PEO model potentially worthwhile.

Winner: Justworks (Significant Premium Savings)

If health insurance is a major expense for your business (typically the case for companies with 10+ employees), the PEO pooling model can deliver significant savings that offset much of the PEO fee.

401(k) & Retirement Plans

Gusto partners with two 401(k) providers:

Gusto does not offer company matching as a built-in benefit — you must fund matching contributions separately if you choose to offer them. Total cost for a 25-employee company:

  • Guideline fees: $49/month + $200/month (25 × $8) = $249/month ($2,988/year)
  • Company match (if offering 3% of $60K average salary): $45,000/year
  • Total: $47,988/year

Justworks: Built-In 401(k) with Company Match (Plus Plan)

Justworks offers a Vanguard 401(k) with automatic enrollment and company matching built into the Plus plan:

  • Justworks provides up to 4% company match (50 cents on the dollar for employee contributions up to 8% of salary)
  • No separate 401(k) provider fees — it's included in the Justworks Plus price
  • Administered through Vanguard with access to low-cost index funds
  • Automatic payroll deductions via Justworks payroll integration

For a 25-employee company with $60K average salaries:

  • Company match (4% of $1.5M total payroll): $60,000/year
  • Justworks Plus includes this as part of the PEO fee (no additional 401(k) admin fees)

The company match is a significant benefit — valued at roughly $2,400 per employee per year if employees max out the match. This is a powerful recruiting and retention tool.

Winner: Justworks (Built-In Company Match on Plus Plan)

If you want to offer company matching without managing a separate 401(k) provider, Justworks Plus delivers more value. If you want flexibility to choose your own 401(k) provider or don't want to offer matching, Gusto gives you more control.

Compliance & Multi-State Support

Gusto: Automated Compliance Tools (You're Responsible)

Gusto automates many compliance tasks:

  • Files and remits federal, state, and local payroll taxes automatically
  • Submits new hire reports to state agencies
  • Generates and e-files W-2s and 1099s at year-end
  • Tracks PTO accruals (if using Gusto PTO management on Plus/Premium)
  • Sends compliance reminders (e.g., workers' comp renewal, I-9 verification)

However, you remain legally responsible for compliance. Gusto provides tools and automation, but if something goes wrong (e.g., a missed tax filing, an incorrect I-9 form), you're liable — not Gusto.

Multi-state payroll: Available on Plus and Premium plans. Gusto automatically registers your business in new states, calculates state and local taxes, and files state unemployment (SUTA) and income tax withholding in each jurisdiction. However, you must ensure your business is registered for state-specific requirements (e.g., California DE-4 forms, New York wage notices).

Justworks: Full-Service Compliance (They're Co-Liable)

Because Justworks is your co-employer, Justworks assumes legal responsibility for payroll tax filings and compliance. The platform handles:

  • All payroll tax filings (federal, state, local) under Justworks' tax ID numbers
  • State registrations and unemployment insurance (SUTA) in all 50 states
  • New hire reporting, I-9 verification, and employment eligibility
  • Workers' comp insurance and claims management
  • FMLA tracking and leave administration
  • ADA accommodations and employment law compliance
  • Termination support and unemployment claims response

Justworks also provides HR advisory services — you can ask questions about employment law, policy development, harassment investigations, and terminations. This is valuable for businesses without an internal HR team.

The trade-off: You have less control over compliance processes. Justworks handles everything, which is convenient but also means you're trusting them to stay compliant on your behalf.

Winner: Justworks (Full-Service Compliance + HR Advisory)

If you operate in multiple states, have complex compliance needs, or want someone else to handle employment law questions, Justworks provides significantly more support. If you're comfortable managing compliance yourself with software tools, Gusto is sufficient and much cheaper.

Workers' Compensation

Gusto: Pay-As-You-Go Add-On (AP Intego)

Gusto offers workers' compensation insurance as an add-on through its partner AP Intego. Key features:

  • Pay-as-you-go pricing: Premiums are automatically deducted from each payroll based on actual wages paid (no upfront deposit or annual estimate required).
  • Automatic coverage adjustments: If you add or remove employees, coverage adjusts automatically.
  • Integrated with payroll: Gusto calculates workers' comp premiums based on employee classifications and payroll data, then debits your account at each payroll run.
  • Available in most states (not available in all states — check eligibility during setup).

You must opt in to Gusto workers' comp — it's not included by default. Pricing varies by industry, job classification, and claims history. For a typical office-based business, workers' comp costs roughly 0.5-2% of payroll. For construction or manufacturing, it can be 5-15% or higher.

Justworks: Built-In Workers' Comp (Included in PEO Fee)

Workers' compensation is automatically included in every Justworks plan. Because Justworks is your co-employer, they secure workers' comp coverage for all employees across all states as part of the PEO service. Key features:

FAQ

Is Gusto or Justworks better for small businesses?

It depends on your priorities. If you want transparent pricing, full control over payroll, and a simple DIY platform, Gusto is better — starting at $49/month + $6/employee with unlimited payroll runs, tax filing, and benefits brokerage built in. If you want white-glove service, access to Fortune 500-level benefits, and someone else handling compliance, Justworks is better — though pricing is custom and typically starts around $99/month + percentage of payroll. For businesses under 10 employees on a tight budget, Gusto is more affordable. For businesses with 10-50 employees that want comprehensive PEO services, Justworks offers more value through better benefits and compliance support.

How much does Justworks cost compared to Gusto in 2026?

Gusto publishes transparent pricing: Simple plan at $49/month + $6/employee, Plus plan at $80/month + $12/employee. Every plan includes full-service payroll, tax filing, and benefits administration. Justworks does not publish pricing publicly — you must request a custom quote. Based on industry data and user reports, Justworks typically charges a percentage of total payroll (often 3-5%) with a per-employee fee ranging from $99 to $180+ per month depending on company size, location, and selected benefits. For a 25-employee company with $1.5M annual payroll, Gusto Plus costs $4,560/year. Justworks typically costs $30,000-$45,000/year for the same scenario — roughly 7-10x more expensive. However, Justworks includes richer benefits (lower premiums through PEO pooling, 401(k) matching, commuter benefits) which can offset some of the cost difference.

What is a PEO and do I need one?

A Professional Employer Organization (PEO) becomes the co-employer of your workforce. Justworks is a PEO; Gusto is not. In a PEO model, Justworks handles all payroll, benefits, taxes, workers' comp, and compliance on your behalf — and your employees technically become employees of Justworks for legal and benefits purposes. This gives your small business access to Fortune 500-level benefits because you're pooled with thousands of other companies. The trade-off is higher cost and less control. You need a PEO if: (1) you want enterprise-grade benefits without negotiating with carriers yourself, (2) you operate in multiple states and want help navigating complex compliance, (3) you prefer outsourcing HR admin entirely. You don't need a PEO if: (1) you're comfortable managing payroll yourself with software, (2) you want to keep costs low, or (3) you prefer direct relationships with benefits carriers and vendors.

Does Justworks handle workers' compensation automatically?

Yes — workers' compensation is built into Justworks and automatically included in every plan. Because Justworks is your co-employer, they secure workers' comp coverage for all employees across all states as part of the PEO service. You don't need to shop for separate workers' comp policies or manage renewals — it's bundled. Gusto also offers workers' compensation, but it's an add-on rather than built-in. Gusto partners with AP Intego to provide pay-as-you-go workers' comp that's automatically included in payroll deductions. You must opt in and set it up separately, but once active, it works similarly to Justworks — no upfront deposits, automatic premium adjustments.

Can Justworks handle international employees?

Justworks does not offer Employer of Record (EOR) services for international employees. The platform is US-focused and built for US payroll, US benefits, and US compliance. If you need to hire full-time employees outside the United States, you'll need a separate EOR platform like Deel or Rippling. Gusto is also US-only for payroll but can pay international contractors in 80+ countries via Gusto Global. For international hiring, neither platform is sufficient on its own — consider Deel or Rippling instead.

Which has better health insurance options — Gusto or Justworks?

Justworks typically offers better health insurance options due to its PEO pooling model. Because Justworks co-employs thousands of workers across hundreds of companies, it can negotiate with carriers (Aetna, Cigna, Kaiser, UnitedHealthcare) for large-group rates that small businesses cannot access independently. Justworks members often report premium savings of 10-30% compared to shopping the small-group market on their own. Gusto acts as a benefits broker — it helps you shop plans and enroll employees, but you're purchasing small-group coverage directly under your own company name. Gusto partners with SimplyInsured and offers access to major carriers, but you won't get PEO pooling discounts. The trade-off: Gusto gives you full control over plan selection, while Justworks limits you to pre-negotiated plan options within the PEO network.

Is Justworks worth the higher cost?

Justworks is worth the higher cost if you value comprehensive benefits and outsourced compliance over cost savings. The platform makes sense for businesses with 10-100 employees that want Fortune 500-level benefits (rich health plans, 401(k) with company match, commuter benefits, FSA/HSA), multi-state compliance support, and white-glove service. The ROI improves when you factor in time saved on HR admin, reduced benefits premiums through PEO pooling, and lower liability risk from compliance errors. However, for bootstrapped startups, single-state businesses under 10 employees, or companies with tight margins, Justworks is usually overkill — Gusto delivers 80% of the value at 20% of the cost. The break-even point tends to be around 20-30 employees, where benefits savings and compliance complexity start to justify the PEO premium.

Can I switch from Gusto to Justworks or vice versa?

Yes, but switching is easier from Justworks to Gusto than the reverse. Moving from a PEO to a non-PEO platform means unwinding the co-employment relationship, which can take 30-90 days and requires careful timing around benefits renewal dates, payroll cycles, and tax year-end. You'll also need to renegotiate health insurance separately (losing PEO pooling rates). Moving from Gusto to Justworks is simpler — Justworks handles most of the migration, including benefits enrollment, payroll setup, and employee data import. Both platforms offer migration support, but expect a 1-2 month transition period either way. Best practice: time the switch to coincide with your benefits renewal (typically January 1) to avoid mid-year disruption.

Gusto vs Justworks isn't really a head-to-head comparison — it's a choice between two fundamentally different approaches to managing your workforce.

Choose Gusto if: You want affordable, transparent payroll software ($49-80/month + per-employee fee), full control over benefits and compliance, and fast self-service setup. Best for startups, small businesses, and companies comfortable managing payroll themselves.

Choose Justworks if: You want white-glove PEO service with Fortune 500-level benefits, lower health premiums through PEO pooling, built-in 401(k) matching, HR advisory support, and outsourced compliance — and you're willing to pay 7-10x more for it. Best for growing businesses with 10-100 employees that value comprehensive benefits and risk reduction over cost savings.

For most businesses under 20 employees, Gusto delivers better value. For businesses with 20-100 employees that prioritize benefits and HR support, Justworks can justify the premium — especially when factoring in health insurance savings and 401(k) matching.
