Gusto vs Justworks (2026)

Affordable payroll software vs PEO with enterprise benefits: which model fits your business?

Updated: March 10, 2026 7-min read

TL;DR

Gusto is traditional payroll software with transparent pricing ($49-180/month base + $6-22/employee) that gives you automated payroll, tax filing, benefits administration, and HR tools. You remain the employer and maintain full control.

Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) that becomes your co-employer ($79-109 per employee monthly, no base fee). This unlocks Fortune 500-level health insurance, built-in workers' comp, and 24/7 HR support from real experts — but costs 3-4x more.

Choose Gusto if you're under 25 employees, budget-conscious, value flexibility and integrations, or don't need premium health insurance. Choose Justworks if you're competing for talent, need better health insurance to recruit, operate in multiple states, or want dedicated HR experts on call.

Real-World Cost Comparison

Scenario 1: 10-employee startup

  • Gusto Plus: $80 + ($12 × 10) = $200/month
  • Justworks PEO Basic: $79 × 10 = $790/month
  • Difference: $590/month ($7,080/year) — Justworks costs 4x more

Scenario 2: 25-employee growing company

  • Gusto Plus: $80 + ($12 × 25) = $380/month
  • Justworks PEO Basic: $79 × 25 = $1,975/month
  • Difference: $1,595/month ($19,140/year) — Justworks costs 5x more

Scenario 3: 50-employee established business

  • Gusto Premium: $180 + ($22 × 50) = $1,280/month
  • Justworks PEO Plus: $109 × 50 = $5,450/month
  • Difference: $4,170/month ($50,040/year) — Justworks costs 4.3x more

When the price gap narrows: If you factor in Gusto's workers' comp add-on (typically $50-200/month depending on industry) and premium health insurance plans, the total cost difference shrinks. For a 25-employee company, adding workers' comp to Gusto brings the monthly cost to approximately $580-680, while Justworks PEO Plus at $2,725/month still costs 4-5x more.

Both platforms handle core payroll excellently. The differences are in control, flexibility, and support.

  • Next-day direct deposit (same-day available for additional fee)
  • Multi-state payroll with automatic state registration and compliance
  • Contractor payments: 1099 contractors paid alongside W-2 employees
  • Garnishment management: Handles wage garnishments, child support, tax levies
  • Time tracking integration: Built-in time tracking for hourly employees, or integrates with TSheets, Homebase, etc.
  • Pay stubs: Employees access digital pay stubs anytime via self-service portal

What users say: "Gusto made payroll as easy as a few clicks. Tax filing happens automatically, and I've never had a compliance issue." — G2 review

Justworks Payroll

  • Unlimited payroll runs included in all PEO plans
  • Automated tax calculations and filing (federal + state)
  • Next-day direct deposit

  • Contractor payments: 1099 contractors handled alongside employees
  • Garnishment management

  • PEO advantage: Because Justworks is the employer of record, they assume more liability for compliance and tax filing errors

What users say: "Payroll is seamless. What really stands out is the compliance peace of mind — Justworks handles everything proactively." — Capterra review

Verdict: Payroll

Winner: Tie — Both platforms excel at payroll automation. Gusto offers slightly more flexibility and better integrations with third-party time tracking tools. Justworks offers more liability protection through the co-employment model, which can be valuable if you're risk-averse or operating in heavily regulated industries.

Real advantage: "We finally offer affordable, quality health benefits to our team. The plans Justworks gets us access to are comparable to what Fortune 500 companies offer." — Justworks user on TrustRadius

Verdict: Benefits

Winner: Justworks — If benefits are a priority (especially health insurance and workers' comp), Justworks is the clear winner. The enterprise-level group rates and bundled workers' comp can save enough money to partially offset the higher monthly fees. Gusto's benefits are solid for basic needs, but don't compare to what a PEO can unlock.

Approach: Gusto gives you the tools to manage HR yourself. It's DIY-friendly with good documentation, but you're still responsible for making HR decisions.

Justworks HR Tools

  • Document management: Centralized employee files

  • Performance management: Basic performance review tools (Plus plan)
  • Compliance support: Proactive compliance alerts for all 50 states — Justworks monitors labor law changes and tells you what to do

  • Unemployment claims: Justworks handles unemployment claims on your behalf
  • Workers' comp claims: Justworks manages the entire process

Approach: Justworks essentially becomes your outsourced HR department. Instead of figuring it out yourself, you call their team and they tell you what to do. This is especially valuable for first-time founders or businesses without dedicated HR staff.

Verdict: HR Tools & Support

Winner: Justworks — If you need hands-on HR guidance, Justworks wins decisively. The 24/7 access to real HR experts is worth the cost for many businesses. Gusto's HR tools are solid for self-sufficient teams, but they don't replace actual HR expertise.

The Bottom Line

Gusto is the smarter choice for most small businesses under 25 employees. It's affordable ($49-180/month base), transparent, easy to use, and handles payroll, taxes, and benefits without complexity. You maintain full control, get access to 100+ integrations, and can manage HR tasks yourself with solid documentation and support.

Justworks justifies its premium cost ($79-109 per employee) if benefits are make-or-break for your hiring strategy. The enterprise-level health insurance, built-in workers' comp, and 24/7 HR support from real professionals can save you money and headaches if you're scaling quickly, operating in multiple states, or competing for talent in competitive industries.

The break-even point typically happens around 15-25 employees when benefits become critical to hiring. Below that range, Gusto's lower costs usually win. Above that range, if your industry demands competitive benefits, Justworks' investment pays off.

Try before you buy: Both platforms offer demos and free trials. Test them with your real workflows, ask about your specific use cases, and run the numbers for your team size. The right choice depends on your business priorities — budget vs. benefits, control vs. outsourced HR, flexibility vs. simplicity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gusto or Justworks better for small businesses?

It depends on your priorities and budget. Gusto is better for small businesses (1-20 employees) focused on affordability and straightforward payroll — starting at $49/month + $6/employee with full payroll, tax filing, and basic benefits included. Justworks is better for small businesses that need competitive health insurance to attract talent — but you'll pay significantly more ($79-$109 per employee monthly). For a 10-employee company, Gusto Plus costs $200/month all-in, while Justworks PEO Basic costs $790/month. That $590 monthly difference buys you enterprise-level health insurance and workers' comp, which may be worth it if you're competing for talent.

How much does Justworks cost compared to Gusto in 2026?

Gusto offers transparent pricing: Simple plan at $49/month + $6/employee, Plus at $80/month + $12/employee, Premium at $180/month + $22/employee. Justworks charges per-employee only: Payroll Plan starts at $50 base + $8/employee, PEO Basic at $79/employee (no base), and PEO Plus at $109/employee (no base). For a 15-employee company: Gusto Plus costs $260/month, while Justworks PEO Basic costs $1,185/month — more than 4x higher. However, Justworks includes workers' comp and access to Fortune 500-level health insurance, which Gusto charges extra for. The price gap narrows when you factor in Gusto's workers' comp add-on and premium health insurance plans.

What is the difference between Gusto and Justworks?

The fundamental difference is in their business models. Gusto is traditional payroll software — you remain the employer, Gusto provides the tools to manage payroll, taxes, and benefits yourself. Justworks is a Professional Employer Organization (PEO) — they become your co-employer through a legal partnership, which unlocks access to enterprise-level health insurance and benefits that small businesses can't typically get alone. Gusto gives you more flexibility, more integrations, and lower costs. Justworks gives you better health insurance rates, built-in workers' comp, and dedicated HR support from real experts, but at a significantly higher monthly cost.

Is Justworks worth the extra cost?

Justworks is worth the premium if you need competitive employee benefits to attract and retain talent, especially in competitive industries like tech, marketing, or finance. If health insurance is a major concern for your team, Justworks' access to Fortune 500-level group plans is genuinely valuable. For a 20-employee startup competing for talent against larger companies, paying an extra $600-800/month for superior health insurance and HR support can be worth it. However, if your team is young, healthy, less benefits-focused, or you're bootstrapping, Gusto's lower costs make more sense. The break-even point typically happens around 15-25 employees when benefits become critical to hiring.

Does Justworks include workers' compensation insurance?

Yes — workers' compensation insurance is included in all Justworks PEO plans (Basic and Plus) at no additional cost. This is one of the major advantages of the PEO model. Gusto does not include workers' comp — it's an optional add-on that costs extra. For businesses in industries with higher workers' comp premiums (construction, manufacturing, healthcare), Justworks' bundled coverage can save significant money. This is a meaningful differentiator when comparing total cost between the two platforms.

Can I leave Justworks if I don't like the co-employment model?

Yes, you can leave Justworks, but switching away from a PEO is more complicated than canceling regular software. Because Justworks is your co-employer, transitioning to a different provider requires transferring employment records, benefits enrollment, payroll history, and potentially re-negotiating health insurance plans. Most PEOs, including Justworks, require 30-day notice before termination. The transition process typically takes 4-8 weeks and can disrupt employee benefits during the switch. This is why it's critical to carefully evaluate whether the PEO model fits your business before signing up — it's harder to undo than switching from Gusto to another payroll provider.

Does Gusto offer PEO services?

No — Gusto does not operate as a PEO. Gusto is traditional payroll software where you remain the legal employer. However, Gusto does offer comprehensive benefits brokerage services through partnerships with major insurance carriers, giving you access to health, dental, vision, 401(k), and other benefits. The difference is that Gusto doesn't become your co-employer, so you don't get the same negotiating leverage for group insurance rates that a PEO like Justworks provides. If you specifically need the PEO model, Justworks, TriNet, Insperity, or Rippling (which offers PEO as an add-on) are better alternatives.

Which platform has better customer support?

Both platforms are well-regarded for customer support, but Justworks has a slight edge. Justworks offers 24/7 HR support on the Plus plan with real HR professionals who understand employment law, compliance, and benefits — essentially giving you an on-demand HR team. Gusto offers phone, email, and chat support on all plans, with priority support and a dedicated customer success manager on the Premium plan. Gusto's response times can slow during peak tax season. On review sites like G2 and Capterra, both platforms score similarly (4.5-4.6 stars for support), but Justworks users consistently highlight the value of having HR experts available for complex questions, while Gusto users appreciate the self-service resource center.
